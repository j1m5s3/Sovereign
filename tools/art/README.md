# Sovereign art pipeline

Claude makes the art with scripts ([leaders-and-art-style.md](../../specs/sovereign/leaders-and-art-style.md),
Art toolchain): every model is generated by Python in Blender, then imported into the
Unreal project by Python. Nothing is hand-modelled and no downloaded geometry ships. The surfaces of
buildings and paving come from CC0 photo scans (Poly Haven), which the build downloads and turns into
the detail sheets, and the people's walk, run and idle are retargeted from CMU motion capture (`mocap/`); their
credits are in [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md). References are for studying shapes only.

```bash
python tools/art/build_art.py            # every kit
python tools/art/build_art.py Classical  # one kit
```

A full build also fetches the scans (`fetch_textures.py`, once, to `art/build/Sources/`; offline, the painted
patterns stand in) and rebuilds the rigged people and horsemen. Needs Blender 5.2 (`BLENDER` overrides the path) and
Unreal Engine 5.8 (`UNREAL_EDITOR_CMD`). The FBX intermediates go to `art/build/` (git-ignored). The
imported `.uasset` files in `unreal/Content/Art/` are committed, so a fresh clone runs
with art. The game falls back to primitives for anything missing.

## Sound

`tools/audio/synth.py` makes every sound by script with numpy (run in Blender's Python, which has it): effects (clicks, the end-turn bell, the founding fanfare, sword clash, bow, musket, cannon, marching feet, hooves, war drums, a new era's swell, victory and defeat), ambience loops (country wind and birds, a town's murmur, a battle's clamour) and five music tracks composed by a seeded modal generator over plucked strings, pipes, pads and frame drums (menu, Ancient, Classical, Medieval, Modern). `tools/audio/ue_import_audio.py` imports them to `unreal/Content/Audio/` (committed); music and ambience loop.

```bash
"C:/Program Files/Blender Foundation/Blender 5.2/blender.exe" -b --factory-startup --python tools/audio/synth.py -- art/build/audio
UnrealEditor-Cmd unreal/Sovereign.uproject -run=pythonscript -script=tools/audio/ue_import_audio.py
```

The game plays them through `FSovAudio` (`SovAudio.*`): the music follows the player's era, the ambience where they are (map, street, battle), effects follow orders carried out and what changed (a new turn, research or a civic done, a new era, the end). Settings has Volume, Music, Ambience and Effects.

## Painted detail

Each kit maps its colours to a pattern (`kitlib.patterns({...})` at the top of the kit; `Piece.wear(...)` overrides it for the next solids). The map is global across kits, so a colour shared by two kits wears one pattern. `Piece.build` stores the pattern's cell (of the 8x8 sheet) in the vertex colour's alpha as `cell / 63` and gives every face UVs by box projection in metres (`kitlib.REPEAT`; a scanned pattern repeats over the metres its scan covers), so detail is the same size on every piece. The material multiplies vertex colour, the team tint and the cell (doubled, so mid grey is no change), and takes the cell's relief from the normal sheet. `portrait.py` renders with the same maths.

Patterns: `plain`, `cloth`, `leather`, `metal`, `skin`, `hair`, `wood`, `stone` (rock), `plaster`, `rooftile`, `foliage`, `thatch`, the map's `water`, `grass` and `sand`, then `masonry` (dressed stone and marble), `brick`, `adobe` (mud brick and clay), `flagstones` and `cobbles`. The scanned ones are listed in `blender/sources.py` with the metres they cover and how strongly their shading and relief show; the colour is taken out of each scan (only its light and shade against the neighbourhood is kept), so the kit's palette still decides the colour. The C++ side paints its own ground with the cells in `SovArt::Cell`.

## Rigged people's motion

The people's WalkSlow, Walk, Run and Idle (`blender/rig_figures.py`, `blender/mocap.py`) are motion capture from the
CMU database (`mocap/README.md`), retargeted onto the one skeleton every figure shares (about 1.75 m tall, legs 84 cm
hip to ankle). They play in place: the game moves the figure, picks the loop by its speed and plays it at that speed
over the speed the loop was authored at, so planted feet hold still (as Epic's Game Animation Sample picks its mocap
by speed):

| Action | Loop | Authored speed | Use at speed v (cm/s) | Play rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WalkSlow (a stroll) | 27 frames at 24 fps (1.13 s, one stride of 113 cm) | 100.4 cm/s | below 125 | v / 100.4 |
| Walk | 25 frames (1.04 s, one stride of 159 cm) | 152.5 cm/s | 125 to 215 | v / 152.5 |
| Run (a jog) | 19 frames (0.79 s, one stride of 234 cm) | 295.2 cm/s | above 215 | v / 295.2 |
| Idle | 166 frames (6.9 s) | 0 | standing | 1 |

Every gait loop (WalkSlow, Walk, Run) starts as the left foot plants (`mocap/README.md`): frame 0 is the first frame
the figure's left foot (the rig's `foot_r`) is planted in each, so the game can blend two of them at the same phase.

A figure scaled by s strides s times as far: compare and divide by the speeds times s. The switches sit where the two
loops' play rates are about equally far from 1 (WalkSlow 1.25x against Walk 0.82x at 125; Walk 1.41x against Run 0.73x at
215). Below about 60 cm/s WalkSlow plays under 0.6x and reads as slow motion, so keep walkers above that.
`rig_figures.py` prints the speeds after it exports, this line exactly:

    rig_figures: authored speeds WalkSlow 100.4 cm/s, Walk 152.5 cm/s, Run 295.2 cm/s, Idle 0.0 cm/s

If a clip changes, change them here and in the game: `kSovMocapWalkSlowSpeed`, `kSovMocapWalkSpeed` and
`kSovMocapRunSpeed` in `unreal/Source/SovereignBridge/Private/SovGait.h`, which `USovLocomotion` plays the blend by in the
street, in battles and on the map. Only a rig without the blend still goes by the old procedural walk's 108 cm/s
(`kWalkSpeed` in `SovStreetScene.cpp`, and `Pace` in `SovBattleScene.cpp`, which is `kSovHorseWalkSpeed` for a horse).
Change them in the same commit as the regenerated `unreal/Content/Art/Rigged/*.uasset`: the new constants with the old
assets, or the new assets with the old constants, make planted feet slide by the difference.

The horsemen (`blender/rig_riders.py`) have no WalkSlow; their gaits are made in the script, not captured: Walk, a
four-beat walk (24 frames, one stride of 92 cm, 92.3 cm/s); Trot, a two-beat trot on diagonal pairs (16 frames, one
stride of 176 cm, 264.0 cm/s, about a live battle's pace); and Run, a transverse gallop on the right lead (12 frames,
one stride of 229 cm, 457.1 cm/s), each starting as the left hind hoof touches down; and a grazing Idle on straight
legs. Each leg is solved exactly so its planted hoof holds still on the ground, flat, at each key, and the barrel rides
on the planted hooves (each end as high as its legs reach at 98.5% of their length, rounded underneath so it never turns
sharply), so it bounces and rocks with the stride (in the trot it bounces twice a stride, highest as each diagonal pair
passes under it, and does not pitch); the actions and the FBX carry two keys a frame (imported at 48 fps, every key
kept), so the game's straight blend from one key to the next keeps a galloping hoof within a few millimetres; every
touchdown falls on a key (and in the trot every lift-off too). The legs are bound solid by solid (each thigh with the
balls of its hip and knee, each cannon with its ankle's ball, each hoof), so no joint tears and nothing of the barrel,
rider or tack rides a leg. The walk lands left hind, left fore, right hind, right fore a quarter stride apart, each
hoof planted for 65% of the stride; the trot left hind with right fore and then right hind with left fore half a stride
later, each pair planted for 6 of its 16 frames (two frames with no hoof down after each); and the gallop left hind,
right hind, left fore, right fore at 0, 3/24, 8/24 and 11/24. All three share only the left hind's touchdown (the walk
and the trot the right hind's too, but not their fores' or how long a hoof stays down), so two of them mixed at one
phase slide the hooves. The game therefore plays each alone at v over its own speed and hands one to the next only in
a narrow band either side of the speed between the two (their geometric mean, plus or minus 10%:
`kSovHorseHandoverBand`): the walk to the trot from 140 to 172 cm/s and the trot to the gallop from 313 to 382, the
rate running on without a jump through each band. A battle's 208 to 299 cm/s is the trot alone at 0.79x to 1.13x; the
map's steady glide is the gallop at up to 1.6x (a long move, held to 3 s, hurries it).

| Horse action | Loop | Authored speed | Alone at speed v (cm/s) | Play rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Walk | 24 frames at 24 fps (1.0 s, one stride of 92 cm) | 92.3 cm/s | below 140 | v / 92.3 |
| Trot | 16 frames (0.67 s, one stride of 176 cm) | 264.0 cm/s | 172 to 313 | v / 264.0 |
| Run (the gallop) | 12 frames (0.5 s, one stride of 229 cm) | 457.1 cm/s | above 382 | v / 457.1 |
| Idle (grazing) | 72 frames (3 s) | 0 | standing | 1 |

`rig_riders.py` checks each rider before it exports it (every bone but the root and `rider_arm` carries vertices; in
Walk, Trot and Run, sampled four times a frame: a planted hoof moves under 0.5 cm along or off the ground, no hoof goes
0.5 cm into it, the loop closes, the left hind touches down at frame 0, the barrel moves under 3 cm or 3 degrees a
frame; and its one-shots, below; `check_rigs.py Rider ...` runs the same) and prints, after it exports, these two
lines exactly:

    rig_riders: authored speeds Walk 92.3 cm/s, Trot 264.0 cm/s, Run 457.1 cm/s, Idle 0.0 cm/s
    rig_riders: one-shot actions Strike 24 fr (blow at 10, 0.417 s), Hit 8 fr, Death 48 fr, DeathBack 48 fr

The game's constants for them are `kSovHorseWalkSpeed`, `kSovHorseTrotSpeed` and `kSovHorseRunSpeed` in
`unreal/Source/SovereignBridge/Private/SovGait.h` (the walk had been taken for 154 cm/s, so its hooves slid).
`USovLocomotion` puts a horse's walk, trot and gallop in the blend's slow-walk, walk and run places. A horseman rigged
before the trot (no Trot) hands its walk to its gallop in a band around 205 cm/s (185 to 226), and one rigged before
the gallop too (no Run) keeps to its walk, as the game falls back to Walk wherever `SovArt::RiggedAnim` finds no
WalkSlow, Trot or Run.

Check the rigs headless (no Unreal) with Blender, or with pip's `bpy` 5.0 (`pip install bpy==5.0.1`, Python 3.11):

```bash
blender -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/art/blender/check_rigs.py -- Townsman Soldier   # no names: every person; horsemen by name (Rider, Rider_Asian...)
python -I tools/art/blender/check_rigs.py Townsman Soldier
```

## One-shot actions (strikes, hits and deaths)

Every rigged person who fights (the Soldier, Archer and Musketeer in each style, the Rifleman, the generic Leader and
the twelve leaders) also gets four one-shot actions for live battles, keyed in the script (`blender/rig_actions.py`)
rather than captured. The townsfolk, Citizen, Herald and Captain never fight and get none. Each figure strikes by its
class (a gun or a bow by the figure's name, the rest by what its +X hand holds), which `rig_actions.strike_class`
decides and `rig_figures.py` prints as it rigs the figure (`class Soldier: spear (held 2.46 m)`):

Reach. The battle stands a man up to 140 cm from the foe he strikes, hips to hips
(`battle/include/sovereign_battle/sim.h`, `kReach`; a ruler strikes at up to 168). A spear's tip lands in that foe at
the blow, 145 cm ahead of the striker's hips, between the foe's chest and his back, and goes no further than 151: held
where it stands at rest, a 2.5 m spear's tip went 80 to 120 cm out through his back, so the hand now holds it nearer the
tip (its hand bone slides along it, below), by however much the figure's own spear needs (65 cm for a 2.3 m spear, up to
1.25 m for Soldier_Asian's 2.86 m one; the 1.5 m staffs of FRANCE and INCA slide 14 cm the other way). A club or a
fist gets only as far as leaning and reaching take it: a fist lands about 81 cm ahead, the crook 107 and the macuahuitl
141 (at a foe's chest), so a punch or a crook falls well short of a foe at 140. Closing that would take a step in,
which a Strike cannot take (it keys the upper body only); it is a known limit, left for a later step
(`.jit/JIT_PLAN.md`, Plan G step 5).

The game (`USovLocomotion`, once PR B of Plan G step 5 in `.jit/JIT_PLAN.md` adds one-shot playback to it) will play a
Strike over the upper body only (`spine` and the bones that hang from it: the head, arms and hands), so it reads the
same over Idle or a walk and no foot slides; a Hit on top of whatever plays (its change from its own frame 0); and a
Death or DeathBack over the whole figure, held at its last frame. The hands are bones of their own (`hand_r`,
`hand_l`: 18 bones, two more than before), running from the wrist along the forearm, and what a hand holds rides its
hand, so a strike turns a spear or a musket at the wrist, and slides a spear back through the fist: the hand bone
moves along the spear's line (keyed in place as well as turn) while the hand itself, on the forearm, stays at the
wrist. At rest a hand lies along its forearm, so the loops are as they were. Every action keys every bone, the root
included, and the place of the hips and both hands (`rig_actions.key_rest`): a bone with no curve would carry one
action's pose into the next in the FBX bake. The FBX keeps every key, as the riders' does: with Blender's default
simplifying, which keys a take loses depends on the pose the armature is in when the export starts (a fall's last
frame, as the once checks leave it), so a fighter's loops came out up to 0.8 cm off the same loops exported from Idle
(and every figure's loops up to about 1 cm off the authored ones). With every key kept each take re-imports exactly as
it was keyed, whatever that pose.

In a fall what the hands hold tips over beside him as he goes down (a spear, staff, club, musket or bow forward or
back at his side, a shield onto its face), turned only by the least turn from how it stands at rest, so it never
sweeps over his head or through his legs. Each key from the crouch (or the stagger) on is laid on the ground, measured
on the posed body (`settle`): the hips raised where anything would go below it; as he topples, lowered until a knee
(or his seat) touches; once down, until his chest lies on it, each leg swung down from its hip until it lies there
too, the head turned up just enough that a hat, crown or crest does not prop him up (or, hanging more than 15 cm
above the ground, as the Rifleman's does over his pack, let fall back to within 4 cm of it), and each wrist lowered
until the arm or what the hand holds lies on it (a long item rolled about its own line to lie under the hand).

In every key of a Strike, a Hit and a fall before he lies the head is turned back toward the chest (and, where that is
not enough, the chest toward the hips) by the least that keeps the seams between the head and the body within 10 cm
(`rig_actions.NECK`, `_neck`), about what a plain neck opens with the face turned onto its side as he lies on it; as
he lies, `settle` chooses the head's turn with the same 10 cm in its score (a turn that opens the neck wider costs
more) and leaves the chest as it is. A quiver or a toga's shoulder worn across the
neck is bound partly to the head (as the loops need), up to 30 cm from its joint, and tore open as the head turned:
31 cm on Archer_American, his face turned 70 degrees as he lay, the top of the quiver lying beside his head. So
Archer_American looks along the arrow with his head turned 23 degrees rather than 30 and falls and lies face down, the
archers with a quiver from the hips to the head bow their heads less in the crouch, and Leader_ROME and PERSIA turn
theirs less as they topple. A quiver's lid at the neck still opens up to those 10 cm, which shows close up only.

Between keys each elbow and knee bends the way the one key bends it, carried along with the arm or leg, rolled
evenly toward the way the next key bends it (round outside the arm where that roll is near half a turn): bending
toward a mix of the two keys' elbows rolled a nearly straight arm half over in a frame. A foot that one key turns
with the shin and the other holds on the ground turns against the shin (flat at the crouch to pointed as the knees
come down): turned between them in the world it flipped over in a frame. Where a foot would still go into the ground
its heel is lifted, as it pivots on its toes, and a wrist is raised, frame by frame.

A robe (the cloth the two skirt bones swing) is rigid: it cannot fold, and swung clear of the ground it would stand up
off a fallen man as a tent, or fold through his legs while he still stood and bare them. So while he stands its skirt
bones swing further only where its cloth would go into the ground (`drape`, at most 15 degrees a frame, found for the
whole take at once so a robe never pops from one shape to another), and once his knees give (frame 14) it goes down
with his legs, into the ground where it must, which hides it, the turns held from the landing so it lies as still as
he does. Nor does it rise more than 30 cm above the hips: falling back he lands on his seat with his knees still up,
and there `drape` holds the robe's front down, which once stood up before his face like a sail. Into the ground it
goes deep: up to 79 cm under a robed leader as he sits down (DeathBack) and 43 cm as he topples (Death), and up to 31
cm when he lies still. The game's camera, looking down, sees only the ground there; from the side the robe is a rigid
cone through the floor, and keeping it out would take cloth that folds (bones of its own, or a simulation). Then the
floor pass lifts the hips by however far anything but the robe (a shield, a quiver, a sword) would still go into the
ground and, from two frames after he lands (frame 26, or 28 falling back), lays him with his lowest point 0.5 cm above
it; the fall is keyed again with that lift in the hips, so the hands of a man on his back stay on the ground while a
quiver or a cloak lifts his body. A hat stays on the head and, beside a fallen man's head, stands on its brim (the
head turned up only as far as keeps it out of the ground): lying flat it would have to come off, on a bone of its own.

`rig_figures.py` prints, after the speeds, this line exactly:

    rig_figures: one-shot actions Strike 24 fr (blow at 10, 0.417 s), Hit 8 fr, Death 36 fr, DeathBack 36 fr

Once PR B has added them, if the blow's frame or the Strike's length changes, change the game's `kSovStrikeImpact`
(10/24) and `kSovStrikeLength` in `unreal/Source/SovereignBridge/Private/SovGait.h` in the same commit as the
regenerated `unreal/Content/Art/Rigged/*.uasset`: otherwise a man flinches before or after the blow that lands on him.

`check_rigs.py` checks the one-shots of every figure that has them, on a line of their own (`check <Name> once
(<class>): ...`), over one lap each, its last frame included:
- in Strike and Hit no foot vertex moves more than 0.1 cm, the last frame is within 0.5 cm of the first and frame 0 is
  the rest pose;
- in Death and DeathBack the feet planted at frame 0 slide no more than 3 cm until the knees buckle (frame 14), the
  fall lies still from frame 28 (every frame from it within 0.2 cm of the last), and it ends with its hips no higher
  than 30 cm, its lowest point (the robe aside) within -1 to +3 cm of the ground, its head on the take's side of its
  hips, and its hips and chest, its legs (the robe aside) and what each hand holds each within 4 cm of the ground (not
  propped up on a hat, a shield's rim or a heel), and no frame of it has the robe more than 30 cm above the hips;
- in every take no vertex goes more than 1 cm into the ground (but a robe in a fall from frame 14, which the ground
  hides), none moves more than 60 cm and no bone turns more than 70 degrees from one frame to the next (but in the
  four frames into a Strike's blow, the fastest thing in it; a spear's or club's wind-up turns the arm up to about 64,
  a foot turned over or an arm rolled half over turned 115 or more) and every bone is keyed;
- in every take the seams between the head and the body open no more than 10.5 cm (`rig_actions.NECK`, which the keys
  keep to, and half a cm between them; reported as `neck`), and in DeathBack the hips' drop never slows by more than
  0.5 cm a frame from one frame to the next before he lands on his seat at 21 (reported as `slows`);
- at the blow a spear or gun is within 20 degrees of forward in plan and 25 of level, a spear's tip 130 to 160 cm
  ahead (its tip in a foe at the battle's 140 cm, and in no frame further than 160 ahead, out through his back) and a
  gun's at least 80 cm; a club's head is at least 90 cm ahead, no higher than 150 cm and moved at least 25 cm over
  the two frames before; a fist is at least 70 cm ahead and moved at least 25 cm (a fist or the crook falls short of
  a foe at 140: see Reach above); a bow is within 25 degrees of upright, its grip 35 cm ahead and 120 to 165 cm up;
- what the hands hold, tested every 2 cm along its edges (a spear's shaft has vertices only at its ends), never comes
  within 11 cm of the chest's line (from the hips' joint to the neck) or 10 cm of the head's bone: in a Strike or Hit
  for a spear, club or fist (the fist's hand bone; a bow's or gun's, held against the body, is only reported), and in
  a fall for anything held, nor then within 7 cm of either thigh's bone;
- in a Strike or Hit no forearm or hand comes more than 1 cm inside the chest (those 11 cm about its line) where it
  was not at frame 0 (an archer's draw hand brought straight down from beside his ear would pass through his belly).

The loops' SWING test (what a hand holds moving more than 15 cm against the chest) does not apply to the one-shots. A
figure that fails only the one-shots' checks, or whose one-shots could not be made or checked at all (an error while
`rig_figures.py` works out what its hands hold and its class, while `rig_actions.add` keys them, or in the once
checks, under another Blender say), is exported without them, and `rig_figures.py` says why: the game then has it
stand through a strike and roll over as it did before, rather than losing the figure. (An error anywhere else, such
as keying the loops, still stops the run.)

Look at them headless with `pose_sheet.py`: per figure, a row per take of its key frames (Strike 0, 6, 8, 10, 13,
17, 20, 23; Hit 0, 2, 5, 8; Death 0, 6, 11, 14, 17, 19, 22, 24, 35; DeathBack 0, 6, 11, 14, 17, 19, 21, 23, 26, 35),
each from the side and three-quarter, on a ground ruled every 50 cm (Workbench, vertex colours); with `--every`, every
frame of each take, three-quarter. The checks catch numbers; the sheet catches a spear held like a broom.

```bash
python -I tools/art/blender/pose_sheet.py art/build/poses.png Soldier Soldier_American Archer Musketeer Rifleman Leader_GREECE Leader_ENGLAND Leader_EGYPT
python -I tools/art/blender/check_rigs.py Soldier Archer Leader_JAPAN
```

### Horsemen

The five riders (`rig_riders.py`) get the same four takes, keyed in the script two keys a frame like their gaits (the
FBX keeps every key, and `ue_import_rigged.py` imports them at 48 fps, so frame N of a take is N/24 s in the game).
The rider has two bones of his own, which the gaits key at rest so they are as they were (every horse bone in Walk,
Trot, Run and Idle bit for bit the same, and the riders' skeletons have two bones more than before: 17 in Blender,
and the FBX adds its `Armature` node above `root`):
- `rider`, on the barrel, from the seat (0, 0, 1.55 m) to the nape (0, 0, 2.05 m), carries his body, arms, head and
  headgear and Rider_African's shield, which hangs on his arm. He leans and rolls on the edge of his seat he leans
  toward (16 cm ahead of or behind it, 10 cm to its side), so his body never sinks into the barrel.
- `rider_arm`, on `rider`, starts at the grip of what his hand holds and runs along it. It carries the lance with its
  point, pennant and tassels, or Rider_Asian's bow and string. It turns about the grip and is keyed with its location
  too, so the lance can slide through the hand and be thrust.
- His legs down the flanks, the saddle, the cloth, a quiver and the tack stay on the barrel.

|---|---|---|---|

`rig_riders.check_once` checks them over every key (two a frame), the last frame included:
- Strike and Hit: no vertex of the horse moves more than 0.1 cm (in a Hit the neck aside); frame 0 is the rest pose
  and the last frame is the first (every bone within 0.01 cm and 0.01 degrees);
- at the blow the lance is within 20 degrees of level and of straight ahead, its point at least 40 cm ahead of the
  horse's nose; a bow is within 25 degrees of upright, its grip 35 cm ahead of the seat and raised 5 cm;
- Death and DeathBack: each sole stays within 0.5 cm until its leg folds (the forelegs from frame 2, the hind legs
  from 12); from 12 the barrel (the middle of its bone) never rises more than 3 cm above the lowest it has been
  (a horse heaved up onto a folded leg as it rolls fails); it lies still from frame 34 (within 0.2 cm of the last
  frame); at the end its lowest point is within -1 to +3 cm of the ground, each leg's lowest point within 3 cm of the
  ground or of the leg straight under it (a leg held up in the air fails), its barrel under 70 cm and it is rolled
  at least 60 degrees onto the take's side;
- every take: what the rider holds, tested every 2 cm along its edges, goes no more than 0.3 cm into the horse's
  neck or head, the rider's legs, the saddle or a shield on his arm (in the falls too, as it drops); nothing goes
  more than 1 cm into the ground, nothing moves more than 60 cm over a frame (measured from every key, so between
  half frames too), and every bone is keyed.

A rider that fails only these, or whose one-shots could not be keyed (one holding nothing in the lance hand, say:
`rider_arm` then carries nothing, which the loops' check allows) or checked (`check_once` raising, under another
Blender say: `rig_riders.py` and `check_rigs.py` report that as its failure, as they do for people), is exported with
its gaits alone, and `rig_riders.py` says why. The game then keeps the old fall: the horse rolled 80 degrees at z 30.

Known and left: lying on its side, the horse rests on the rider's leg on that side and on its head (his legs ride
the barrel, as in the gaits), so its barrel lies about 16-17 cm off the ground along its length (its lowest vertex
16.3 cm up at frame 48; rolled further, past about 100 degrees, the flat saddle cloth props it instead); the neck's
sideways bend that lays the head on the ground stretches the barrel's top front, which rides the neck vertex by
vertex as on main (about 23 cm on its longest edge, against 4 cm in Idle). At rest the kit's lance
(`kit_figures._lance`) does not pass through the hand (its line is 39.5 cm from the fist's centre, 43 cm ahead of it
at the hand's height), so it is hoisted (0-2.5) and slid home (21.5-24) with no hand on it: the hand takes it at
2.5-4 and lets go of it at 19-21. It cannot take it sooner, as its butt rests under the saddle cloth's overhang
(which reaches 35 cm ahead of the seat and 38 cm out to the side, past the lance) and in front of his knee: the
lance's line can come to the fist only once the butt is up past the cloth's front edge (about frame 2), and bringing
the line to the fist first drives the butt 2.3-2.5 cm into the cloth. On Rider_African, Rider_MiddleEast and
Rider_American the lance at rest also passes 0.8-1.9 cm into a flank cloth panel on the barrel (a thin box at y
about -29 to -33 cm, kit geometry); it goes about as far through it as it slides home (Strike, 23.5) and as it is let
go (Death and DeathBack, 5-6, up to 2 cm). `check_once` does not test that panel, since frame 0, the kit's rest
pose, is already inside it; at a game's distance it cannot be seen.

## Conventions

- Metres in Blender, pivot at the base centre, fronts (doors, faces) face −Y in Blender, which is +Y in Unreal after import (place pieces with that in mind).
- Material slot 0 is the piece's own colours; slot 1 (`Piece.team_color()`) is team colour, tinted per instance by `SovArt::SetKitMesh`. `ue_import.py` deletes and re-creates assets so slot changes take effect.
- Owner colours never go into a mesh: white parts (banner cloth, later figures' tunics) take the material's `Tint`.
- A piece's name is `SM_<Name>`; the game finds it as `/Game/Art/<Kit>/SM_<Name>` (`SovArt::Mesh`). Add new names to `SovArt::RequiredAssets` so the asset test covers them.
