# Sovereign art pipeline

Claude makes the art with scripts ([leaders-and-art-style.md](../../specs/sovereign/leaders-and-art-style.md),
Art toolchain): every model is generated by Python in Blender, then imported into the
Unreal project by Python. Nothing is hand-modelled and no downloaded geometry ships. The surfaces of
buildings and paving come from CC0 photo scans (Poly Haven), which the build downloads and turns into
the detail sheets, and the people's walk, run and idle are retargeted from CMU motion capture (`mocap/`); their
credits are in [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md). References are for studying shapes only.

```bash
python tools/art/build_art.py            # every kit
python tools/art/build_art.py Classical  # one kit
```

A full build also fetches the scans (`fetch_textures.py`, once, to `art/build/Sources/`; offline, the painted
patterns stand in) and rebuilds the rigged people and horsemen. Needs Blender 5.2 (`BLENDER` overrides the path) and
Unreal Engine 5.8 (`UNREAL_EDITOR_CMD`). The FBX intermediates go to `art/build/` (git-ignored). The
imported `.uasset` files in `unreal/Content/Art/` are committed, so a fresh clone runs
with art. The game falls back to primitives for anything missing.

## Sound

`tools/audio/synth.py` makes every sound by script with numpy (run in Blender's Python, which has it): effects (clicks, the end-turn bell, the founding fanfare, sword clash, bow, musket, cannon, marching feet, hooves, war drums, a new era's swell, victory and defeat), ambience loops (country wind and birds, a town's murmur, a battle's clamour) and five music tracks composed by a seeded modal generator over plucked strings, pipes, pads and frame drums (menu, Ancient, Classical, Medieval, Modern). `tools/audio/ue_import_audio.py` imports them to `unreal/Content/Audio/` (committed); music and ambience loop.

```bash
"C:/Program Files/Blender Foundation/Blender 5.2/blender.exe" -b --factory-startup --python tools/audio/synth.py -- art/build/audio
UnrealEditor-Cmd unreal/Sovereign.uproject -run=pythonscript -script=tools/audio/ue_import_audio.py
```

The game plays them through `FSovAudio` (`SovAudio.*`): the music follows the player's era, the ambience where they are (map, street, battle), effects follow orders carried out and what changed (a new turn, research or a civic done, a new era, the end). Settings has Volume, Music, Ambience and Effects.

## Painted detail

Each kit maps its colours to a pattern (`kitlib.patterns({...})` at the top of the kit; `Piece.wear(...)` overrides it for the next solids). The map is global across kits, so a colour shared by two kits wears one pattern. `Piece.build` stores the pattern's cell (of the 8x8 sheet) in the vertex colour's alpha as `cell / 63` and gives every face UVs by box projection in metres (`kitlib.REPEAT`; a scanned pattern repeats over the metres its scan covers), so detail is the same size on every piece. The material multiplies vertex colour, the team tint and the cell (doubled, so mid grey is no change), and takes the cell's relief from the normal sheet. `portrait.py` renders with the same maths.

Patterns: `plain`, `cloth`, `leather`, `metal`, `skin`, `hair`, `wood`, `stone` (rock), `plaster`, `rooftile`, `foliage`, `thatch`, the map's `water`, `grass` and `sand`, then `masonry` (dressed stone and marble), `brick`, `adobe` (mud brick and clay), `flagstones` and `cobbles`. The scanned ones are listed in `blender/sources.py` with the metres they cover and how strongly their shading and relief show; the colour is taken out of each scan (only its light and shade against the neighbourhood is kept), so the kit's palette still decides the colour. The C++ side paints its own ground with the cells in `SovArt::Cell`.

## Rigged people's motion

The people's WalkSlow, Walk, Run and Idle (`blender/rig_figures.py`, `blender/mocap.py`) are motion capture from the
CMU database (`mocap/README.md`), retargeted onto the one skeleton every figure shares (about 1.75 m tall, legs 84 cm
hip to ankle). They play in place: the game moves the figure, picks the loop by its speed and plays it at that speed
over the speed the loop was authored at, so planted feet hold still (as Epic's Game Animation Sample picks its mocap
by speed):

| Action | Loop | Authored speed | Use at speed v (cm/s) | Play rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WalkSlow (a stroll) | 27 frames at 24 fps (1.13 s, one stride of 113 cm) | 100.4 cm/s | below 125 | v / 100.4 |
| Walk | 25 frames (1.04 s, one stride of 159 cm) | 152.5 cm/s | 125 to 215 | v / 152.5 |
| Run (a jog) | 19 frames (0.79 s, one stride of 234 cm) | 295.2 cm/s | above 215 | v / 295.2 |
| Idle | 166 frames (6.9 s) | 0 | standing | 1 |

Every gait loop (WalkSlow, Walk, Run) starts as the left foot plants (`mocap/README.md`): frame 0 is the first frame
the figure's left foot (the rig's `foot_r`) is planted in each, so the game can blend two of them at the same phase.

A figure scaled by s strides s times as far: compare and divide by the speeds times s. The switches sit where the two
loops' play rates are about equally far from 1 (WalkSlow 1.25x against Walk 0.82x at 125; Walk 1.41x against Run 0.73x at
215). Below about 60 cm/s WalkSlow plays under 0.6x and reads as slow motion, so keep walkers above that.
`rig_figures.py` prints the speeds after it exports, this line exactly:

    rig_figures: authored speeds WalkSlow 100.4 cm/s, Walk 152.5 cm/s, Run 295.2 cm/s, Idle 0.0 cm/s

If a clip changes, change them here and in the game: `kSovMocapWalkSlowSpeed`, `kSovMocapWalkSpeed` and
`kSovMocapRunSpeed` in `unreal/Source/SovereignBridge/Private/SovGait.h`, which `USovLocomotion` plays the blend by in the
street, in battles and on the map. Only a rig without the blend still goes by the old procedural walk's 108 cm/s
(`kWalkSpeed` in `SovStreetScene.cpp`, and `Pace` in `SovBattleScene.cpp`, which is `kSovHorseWalkSpeed` for a horse).
Change them in the same commit as the regenerated `unreal/Content/Art/Rigged/*.uasset`: the new constants with the old
assets, or the new assets with the old constants, make planted feet slide by the difference.

The horsemen (`blender/rig_riders.py`) have no WalkSlow; their gaits are made in the script, not captured: Walk, a
four-beat walk (24 frames, one stride of 92 cm, 92.3 cm/s), and Run, a transverse gallop on the right lead (12 frames,
one stride of 229 cm, 457.1 cm/s), each starting as the left hind hoof touches down, and a grazing Idle on straight
legs. Each leg is solved exactly so its planted hoof holds still on the ground, flat, at each key, and the barrel rides
on the planted hooves (each end as high as its legs reach at 98.5% of their length, rounded underneath so it never turns
sharply), so it bounces and rocks with the stride; the actions and the FBX carry two keys a frame (imported at 48 fps,
every key kept), so the game's straight blend from one key to the next keeps a galloping hoof within a few millimetres.
The legs are bound solid by solid (each thigh with the balls of its hip and knee, each cannon with its ankle's ball,
each hoof), so no joint tears and nothing of the barrel, rider or tack rides a leg. Only the left hind's touchdown is
shared: the walk lands left hind, left fore, right hind, right fore a quarter stride apart, the gallop left hind, right
hind, left fore, right fore at 0, 3/24, 8/24 and 11/24, so the two mixed at one phase slide every hoof. The game
therefore hands the walk to the gallop between 92.3 and 138 cm/s (`kSovHorseHandover`, 1.5 times the walk) and above
that plays the gallop alone at v / 457.1: a battle's 208 to 299 cm/s is the gallop at 0.46x to 0.65x, its hooves held.
`rig_riders.py` checks each rider before it exports it (every horse bone carries vertices; in Walk and Run, sampled four
times a frame: a planted hoof moves under 0.5 cm along or off the ground, no hoof goes 0.5 cm into it, the loop closes,
the left hind touches down at frame 0, the barrel moves under 3 cm or 3 degrees a frame; `check_rigs.py Rider ...` runs
the same) and prints, after it exports, this line exactly:

    rig_riders: authored speeds Walk 92.3 cm/s, Run 457.1 cm/s, Idle 0.0 cm/s

The game's constants for them are `kSovHorseWalkSpeed` and `kSovHorseRunSpeed` in
`unreal/Source/SovereignBridge/Private/SovGait.h` (the walk had been taken for 154 cm/s, so its hooves slid). A
horseman rigged before the gallop has no Run, so the game keeps it to its walk, as it falls back to Walk wherever
`SovArt::RiggedAnim` finds no WalkSlow or Run.

Check the rigs headless (no Unreal) with Blender, or with pip's `bpy` 5.0 (`pip install bpy==5.0.1`, Python 3.11):

```bash
blender -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/art/blender/check_rigs.py -- Townsman Soldier   # no names: every person; horsemen by name (Rider, Rider_Asian...)
python -I tools/art/blender/check_rigs.py Townsman Soldier
```

## Conventions

- Metres in Blender, pivot at the base centre, fronts (doors, faces) face −Y in Blender, which is +Y in Unreal after import (place pieces with that in mind).
- Material slot 0 is the piece's own colours; slot 1 (`Piece.team_color()`) is team colour, tinted per instance by `SovArt::SetKitMesh`. `ue_import.py` deletes and re-creates assets so slot changes take effect.
- Owner colours never go into a mesh: white parts (banner cloth, later figures' tunics) take the material's `Tint`.
- A piece's name is `SM_<Name>`; the game finds it as `/Game/Art/<Kit>/SM_<Name>` (`SovArt::Mesh`). Add new names to `SovArt::RequiredAssets` so the asset test covers them.
