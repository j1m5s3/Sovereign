## Plan A: city sites and expansion (done)

At turn 200 the AI held about 7.7 cities a civ on a Small map, where Civ VI usually reaches 10–12.

**Diagnosis** (`sites` scratch tool, seeds 1–2):
- Land is not short: half the passable land is still unowned at turn 200, and 170 plots are still legal city sites.
- The settle scorer's threshold is not the limit: nearly every revealed legal site scores 150 or more.
- Civs reveal little of the map: 0–60 legal sites each at turn 50.
- Some civs see dozens of good sites and still train no Settler after turn 120, because Settlers lost weight then (600 → 400).

**Experiments** (8 seeds, turn 200, against main after #197):

| Build | cities | pop | techs | era | science | culture | prod |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| main | 7.7 | 45.6 | 28.9 | 2.9 | 69.2 | 49.7 | 95.8 |
| S1 Scouts, wider exploring | 6.8 | 40.7 | 28.5 | 2.8 | 62.4 | 45.9 | 85.5 |
| S1b Scouts only explore wider | 6.9 | 40.9 | 28.5 | 2.7 | 66.3 | 48.8 | 89.0 |
| S2 expansion weight to turn 200 (kept) | 8.3 | 46.3 | 29.0 | 2.9 | 67.2 | 49.0 | 95.7 |
| S1b + S2 | 7.6 | 42.9 | 28.4 | 2.7 | 65.1 | 47.3 | 91.0 |

**Kept:** S2. Settlers keep their expansion weight until turn 200 (`kEarlyTurns` 120 → 200), giving +0.6 cities.

**Dropped:** more scouting. Building a Scout early delays the first Settlers, and wider exploring lost cities in every variant.

## Plan B: ready for a playtest (active)

The game can be played end to end from the Unreal menu, but the map and controls make a playtest hard (unreal/README.md "Not yet", checked against the code on 2026-10-07).

1. **Done: map.** Borders, rivers, resources and improvements are drawn (roads already were), with a tile tooltip that includes yields.
2. **Done: saves.** `F5` and `F9` quicksave and quickload in local games, and the main menu can continue the four latest saves (`FSovSession::LoadLocal`).
3. **Done: panels.** The unit and city panels already existed, and `U` already offered promotions. The unit panel now lists promotions held; the city panel shows growth (turns to grow), housing, amenities and its buildings.
4. **Done: yields and help.** `F3` toggles yields on the viewer's plots; `F1` opens a how-to-play screen, hinted at in the status line.
5. **Playtest pass.**
   - **Done: automated playthrough.** `Sovereign.Bridge.HumanSeatPlaysLongGame` plays seat 0 as a newcomer for two games to turn 250 through what the controller offers, saving and resuming every 50 turns. It found no End Turn dead end. It did find that a game had no way out: there was no in-game menu, so after a victory or elimination the only exit was closing the window. `Esc` with nothing selected now opens the menu over the game (resume, save, continue, new game, quit). `F2` was missing from the README.
   - Seed 7's newcomer, who fortifies every unit, lost its only city (London, population 3) to AI seat 3 on turn 129. That is the AI taking a weak one-city neighbour, not a bug.
   - **Next: James plays.** A hands-on game from the menu; fix what he reports.
6. **Done: east-west wrap drawn.** The map actor draws a copy of itself a map's width to the west and east (`ASovMapActor::SyncGhosts`). The camera's focus stays within one copy, so panning past an edge carries on into the other. `LookAt` and the HUD's labels use the copy nearest the camera (`SovHex::NearestCopy`), and rivers, borders and roads across the wrap are drawn rather than skipped. Picking already wrapped through `HexGrid::normalize`. Checked in the game at column 0 of seed 7.

## Plan C: player retention (active)

The decided feature list in `specs/sovereign/player-retention.md` (James, 2026-10-04), in this order: what a playtest feels first, then what builds on the player profiles already carried between games.

1. **Done: the leader's goals at turn end (§4).** Core: `Game::leaderGoals(player)`, the next few personal goals, most pressing first: a promotion to take or close by, an assassin reported in place, a city near revolt, a rival leader within reach. The HUD shows the first two beside the turn line. Test: `leader_goals_put_the_most_pressing_first`.
2. **Done: rivals who remember you (§1).** Each AI civ's memory of a human (`RivalMemory`: games, wars, betrayals, rulers taken each way, cities lost, turns as friends) comes in through the setup from `<name>.rivals.txt` beside the play profile. This game's part is tallied each world turn (`processRivals`; battles now record `EventKind::LeaderLost`), merged and written when the game is saved or ends.
   - Effects stay inside the rules. A grudge (up to 30) and respect (up to 15) form the `PastGames` opinion reason. A grudge lowers the margin the AI needs to declare war on that human, and favours them as a target, by up to 30%. A leader beaten twice reads that human's play as at King. The diplomacy persona lists the history, and the scripted voice taunts or remembers lost crowns.
   - The menu toggles `Rivals remember you` (`-SovNoRivals`) and has `Forget your rivals`. Tests: `rivals_*` in test_profile, `the_persona_remembers_earlier_games`. Save version 80; golden hash regenerated.
3. **Done: chronicle and Hall of Sovereigns (§2).** Chronicle-worthy events are kept all game (`GameState::chronicle`, capped at 1000) and read out as plain lines (`Game::chronicleLines`). That covers wars, peace, friendships, assassinations, rulers captured or slain in battle (`LeaderLost`), successions (new `Succession` event), rebellions, historic moments and new ages. `F4` shows them. `F6`, and the game's end, have them written up off the game thread by the local model under a chronicler prompt, or by `scriptedChronicle` (`Saved/Sovereign/Chronicles/`). At the end the reign's `hallEntry` (ruler, gear, level, promotions, outcome, rivals made) is added to `Saved/Sovereign/Hall.txt`, which the menu shows. Battle replays are left for later. Save version 81.
4. **Done: shorter games (§5).**
   - **Short Reign** is a game speed, `GAMESPEED_SHORT_REIGN` (20% costs, 100 turns; `data/rules/setup.json`). Everything that already scales with speed (costs, era score, the World Congress interval, the turn limit for Score) follows it. Bench, 4 seeds, Small, 4 civs: by turn 100 it reaches era 7.4, 65 techs and 10.8 cities. Standard reaches era 7.9, 73 techs and 12.6 cities at turn 500.
   - **Era starts:** `GameSetup::startEra` and `eraStarts` in `setup.json`, from game-setup.md: Advanced start eras, the non-AI starting units, and the City Center starting buildings. Every civ and city-state gets the earlier eras' techs and civics without their moments, plus their envoys. Majors get the era's gold, faith and units on top of the Settler and Warrior. The world starts in that era, and cities founded during the game start with the era's population and City Center buildings (`Game::applyEraStart`, `applyFoundCity`).
   - Under ASan, every era start runs 25 AI turns cleanly, and a Renaissance Short Reign plays to a turn-93 Diplomatic victory. This found and fixed a write past `momentEras` in `awardFirst` when a moment fell outside its era window.
   - The menu chooses Length and Begin in (`-SovSpeed=`, `-SovEra=`); `sovsim` takes `--speed` and `--era`. Save version 82.
5. **Done: mods (§6).** A mod is a folder with a `mod.json` and rules files, found in the repo's `mods/` or `Saved/Sovereign/Mods/` (`SovMods`) and laid over `data/rules`. Rows replace, `delete`, or (new) `patch` the row of the same id. `GameSetup::mods` names them, so a save reloads with its mods (`peekSaveSetup`), and the online handshake already refused a different mod list (the session now sends it). The menu has a `Mods` list (`Saved/Sovereign/mods.txt`, `-SovMods=a,b`). Example: `mods/swift-settlers`; format in `mods/README.md`. Save version 83.
6. **Done: weekly challenge (§3), the local parts.** `weeklyChallenge(rules, week)` gives the week's setup and goal: a Small map, Short Reign, Prince, an Ancient, Classical or Medieval start, four civs from the week's hash, no carried profile, rivals or mods. Goals are any victory, no war declared, Science, Culture, or score. `checkChallenge` is the board's check. It needs the week's setup and replays the log from it. It rejects any live-battle result outside the band (the game now records results already clamped) and any log that doesn't reach the saved game. Then it judges the goal, turns and score. The menu offers this week's challenge with your last result; at the end the result goes to `Saved/Sovereign/Challenges.txt` and the game is saved as `challenge week N` to submit. The server board itself is not built.
7. **Done: cosmetic unlocks (§7).** Nine achievements (`achievements` in `setup.json`): any victory, one per victory kind (Science, Culture, Domination, Diplomatic), a ruler taken in battle, ruler level 4, winning a Short Reign, and five wonders. `Game::achievementsEarned` checks them. When a game ends, new ones join `Saved/Sovereign/Achievements.txt` and are announced. Each unlocks a `cosmetics` colour for your ruler's figure, chosen on the menu (`Ruler's colour`) and applied only to the viewer's map drawing, never the game state. Loadout skins, banners and city styles wait for art.

8. **Done: battle replays (§2).** Each live battle is recorded as it is fought: the field (spec, colours, woods, walls) and a snapshot every 0.1 s (`FSovBattleRecording`). It is saved to `Saved/Sovereign/Battles/*.sovbattle` when the result goes in. The menu's `Battle replays` (or `-SovReplay=<file>`, so a file can be shared) plays one back through the online remote view, with or without a game running. Checked in the game.

Plan C is done. Only the server side of the challenge board is left.
## Plan D: the player-facing UI (done, 2026-10-09)

Today the game is played through a text HUD and numbered lists (1-9 pick, 0 next page) behind keyboard shortcuts; only the menu, diplomacy, chat and hot-seat screens are widgets. Plan D replaces that with a clickable interface in Slate (C++ widgets, as the menu and diplomacy screen already are), styled from one style set, with a generated icon set. Every key keeps working; buttons press the same keys.

1. **Done: framework, top bar, unit panel, choosers, end turn.** Built as `SSovGameUI` (`SovGameUI.*`) with `FSovStyle` (`SovStyle.*`). The icons are dynamic brushes, because Slate only preloads images for styles registered at start-up. Buttons are not focusable, so Space still ends the turn. The bridge module builds without unity files (helpers in anonymous namespaces clashed). Checked in the game: icons, the research and production panels, founding a city from the unit panel's key, and the end-turn blocker text. `FSovStyle` (colours, fonts, icon brushes from PNGs in `unreal/Content/Slate/Icons/`, drawn by `tools/ui/icons.py` in Blender). One root widget over the map. A top bar: yields per turn with icons, gold and faith banks, favor, tourism, turn and era, research and civic in progress (click to choose), government and menu. A unit panel: name, health bar, moves, strength, level and XP, and a button for each action the unit has. Every chooser as a clickable, scrolling list. An end-turn button that names what blocks the turn and opens it.
2. **Done: city panel.** Replaces the city text when one of your cities is selected: name, capital, population, health bar and loyalty; yields per turn; growth (turns, food, a bar) with housing and amenities; what it builds (icon, turns, progress; click to choose) with buy buttons for gold and faith (shown when a price exists, enabled when affordable); the citizens' focus (seven buttons); the queue, buildings, districts with specialists, religion, power, Benevolence and Fear timers; buttons for production, Benevolence, Fear, governors and the plot yields (F3, Shift+click a plot to lock a citizen). Checked in the game after founding a city.
3. **Done: tech and civic trees.** T and C (and the top bar buttons) open the whole tree (`SSovTreeView`, `SovTreeView.*`) in place of the list: one column group per era, each node after its prerequisites with lines between them, its turns, progress, boost (earned in green) and what it unlocks (units, buildings, districts, improvements, projects, governments, policies, gear, revealed resources). Clicking an open node starts it; clicking a later one makes it the goal, and the controller starts the cheapest open step toward it whenever nothing is in progress (goals are per player, kept on the client). The number keys still pick from the hidden list. Checked in the game: both trees, and picking Pottery.
4. **Done: notifications and tooltips.** A feed above the end-turn button (`SovEvents.*` words the events for both it and the HUD): cities needing production first (click selects the city and opens its list), then up to six events from the last two turns that the player hears of. A click opens the matching screen (deals and war: diplomacy; great people; World Congress; moments: the chronicle) and dismisses it; X dismisses without opening. The HUD no longer prints those events while the widgets are up. The top bar's science, culture, gold and faith tooltips break the total down by city, with the rest as upkeep or modifiers. Checked in the game: the production notice after founding a city.
5. **Done: setup, end-of-game and settings screens.** *Setup* (`SSovSetupScreen`, from the menu's New game (was Single player); `-SovSetup` opens it at launch): the twelve civs and Random, each with its leader and leaning; the civ ability, leader ability, uniques, agenda and heirs in words (new `text` and `uniquesText` fields in `civilizations.json`, read into `CivAbility::text` and `CivType::uniquesText`; the rules checksum in the golden file moved, the state hash did not); map size, civilizations, difficulty, length, start era, natural disasters, Barbarian Clans, Monopolies, rival memory and a fresh map seed. `FSovSetup` gained `Civ`, `Disasters`, `bClans` and `bMonopolies` (`-SovCiv=`); seat 0 takes the picked civ and the others the roster without it. Up/Down/Enter/Esc work there too. *End of game* (in `SSovGameUI`): Victory or Defeat, who won and how, every civ's score and the player's chronicle, with Look at the map, Write the chronicle (F6) and Main menu; also when the player is eliminated. *Settings* (`SSovSettingsScreen`, from the menu; `-SovSettings`): graphics quality, window mode, resolution, vsync, frame limit and interface scale (kept in GameUserSettings, the scale restored at start), and the controls as a reference list. Not done: sound (there is none yet) and key rebinding (keys are fixed in the controller). Checked in the game: picking Egypt by keys and starting as Egypt; the end screen after an AI-only Short Reign duel; the settings screen.
6. **Done: lenses and minimap.** `SovLens.*` recolours the revealed plots for one lens and fills a legend: religion (each city's majority, one colour per religion), loyalty (red to green by the city's loyalty), appeal (five bands), settler (where `canFoundCityAt` allows a city) and trade (the viewer's routes and their cities). The lens is this machine's view, kept in the subsystem; the game mode applies it when it redraws the map. Lens buttons sit above the minimap (click again to clear), and F7 steps through them. The minimap (`SSovMinimap`, `SovMinimap.*`) shows every revealed plot in its owner's colour (or the lens's), fogged plots darker, with a frame where the camera looks; a click or drag moves the camera. Checked in an AI-only duel: the minimap, and the religion, loyalty and settler lenses with their legends.

## Plan E: UI polish (done except sound, 2026-10-09)

With plan D done, the screenshots still show the canvas HUD's status block (a dozen lines of text over the map's top-left) and the gaps step 5 noted. I picked these next, most visible first (standing consent, 2026-10-05).

1. **Done: Empire panel.** `SovStatus.*` turns the empire's standing into lines (identity, faith and religion, era and age, favor and the Congress, the space race, alliances, quests, competitions, emergencies, war weariness, arsenals, climate, governors, offers, envoys, trade routes, the leader, dedications, reputation, interregnum). The canvas HUD prints them only without the widgets; with them, the Empire panel (top bar button or `F8`) shows them, so the map's top-left stays clear. Notices now also ask for what waits on the player: an offer (N), an empty or captive throne (H), a pantheon (I), dedications (F2), governor titles (Z) and envoys (O); the feed shows the eight most pressing. Checked in an AI-only duel.
2. **Done: key rebinding.** `SovKeys.*`: the controller still reads each order key by its default, and `Pressed` asks `SovKeys::Physical` which key triggers it now. Movement, digits, Esc, Enter, Space, Tab, Home, modifiers and the mouse stay fixed. Bindings are saved in GameUserSettings (`[Sovereign.Keys]`) and loaded at start. Tests: `Sovereign.Bridge.KeyRebindingSwaps`, and `LensesTintTheMirror` for plan D step 6. Also fixed: the settings screen read the saved window mode, not the live one, so Apply could switch a windowed game to windowed fullscreen. 3. **Done: plot and unit tooltips in Slate.** The plot under the cursor shows in a styled box beside it (`SovPlotTooltip`, now also listing the units the viewer sees there with HP and strength), only when the cursor is over the map: `SSovGameUI::Tick` checks that no panel of ours is under it. The canvas tooltip is drawn only without the widgets. Needs James's mouse to check: posted mouse moves do not reach the engine's cursor.

5. **Done: the main menu restyled.** The menu uses the game's style (panel, buttons, fonts) over a dimmed backdrop; `New game` (the setup screen) comes first in gold; the difficulty, length, era and rival rows are captioned as being for hot seat and online games; long labels (the weekly challenge) wrap.

The labels read the mirror the map was last drawn from (kept in the subsystem) instead of building a new mirror every frame.

11. **Done: the production list.** Chooser lines can carry an icon, a section and a value on the right (`FChoice::Icon/Section/Right`, `FSovUIChoice`); the production list uses them: districts, buildings, wonders, units and projects in their own sections with turns on the right, then buying with gold and with faith (prices on the right), tiles, and the city. Other lists show as before.
