## Milestones (one PR each)

1. **Done: Pace benchmark and the economic planner:**
   - A benchmark: `sovsim --bench` plays several seeds and prints per-checkpoint averages (turns 50/100/150/200/300: cities, population, techs, civics, science, culture, production, gold banked, era) so every change is measured. A test pins a floor so pace cannot silently regress.
   - Check the core's yield rules the pace rests on (growth, science per citizen, boosts, housing) against the specs before tuning the AI around them.
   - Economic planner per 10-ai Recommended architecture: settling by StandardSettlePlot with the minimum site value and decay, settlers early while good sites remain (Rapid Expansion), builders on the best unimproved worked tiles and boost triggers, citizen management with a food floor, research and civics scored by unlocks x yield bias x boost progress (chasing boosts), production by yield value per cost with needs (housing, amenities, threat), gold spent by the DefaultSavings split (units, slush fund, plots) instead of hoarded.
2. **Done: Grand strategy:** the 16 strategies with their conditions (victory strategies, Early Exploration, Rapid Expansion, Naval, Wonder Obsessed, Dark Age, one per era) re-evaluated every few turns; active strategies shift research, civic, production, diplomacy and military weights; leaders' agendas add their own weights.
3. **Done (this PR): Military layer:** threat and opportunity influence maps; operations (attack city, attack walled city with siege, camp clearing, city defence, escorted settlers, naval superiority) launched at their strength ratios and minimum odds; tactical moves that focus fire, retreat to heal and avoid ending exposed; upgrades.
4. **Difficulty:** Settler..Deity in the setup. Per leader doc §10, levels scale skill first (how far the planner looks ahead, boost chasing from Warlord, Rapid Expansion off at Warlord and below, operation quality, how much player modelling it will use later); the Civ yield, combat, XP, free-boost and starting-unit bonuses apply only at Immortal and Deity. Human bonuses at Settler and Chieftain and the barbarian distance and throttle follow 00-overview. Unreal launch option and HUD.

Player modelling (leader doc §10 layer 2) is the plan after this one.

## Decisions (Claude's recommendations; James gave standing consent)

- Milestone 1 as built: `sovsim --bench N` (seeds 1..N, all-AI, averages at turns 25..500) and `ai::measurePace`; `tests/test_ai_pace.cpp` pins a Duel floor about 25% under the measured pace. Core check: growth, housing bands, science and culture per citizen match the specs, but the core had only seven specialty districts, so housing capped cities; added Aqueduct (placement next to the center and water or a Mountain; housing up to 6, or +2 with fresh water), Neighborhood and Preserve (housing by plot appeal; `Game::plotAppeal` from terrain, feature, improvement, district, wonder, camp and river contributions), Entertainment Complex and Water Park (+1 amenity, exclusive), Dam (Floodplains on a river; housing 3, amenity 1, prevents floods and droughts), Aqueduct prevents droughts, Government Plaza and Diplomatic Quarter (one per player; their buildings carry yields only). Aerodrome, Spaceport and Canal wait for air units, the space race and canal movement. This also brings in the five wonders that need these districts (52 wonders). AI: settlers by free sites near our cities (no turn cutoff, more at once, weighted up before turn 120) and not blocked by city-state wars; city-states are war targets only once no site is free; production value per cost loses value in proportion beyond 12 turns of the city's production; buildings and districts revalued against units (yield x 25, districts 100 + 40 per great person point + adjacency x 25, housing and amenity needs); culture weighs like science; research values buildings by their yields and military units less in peace; no new army while gold per turn is negative in peace; gold buys the best-yield building above a smaller reserve; settlers and builders are bought at once. Benchmark, Small map, 4 civs, seeds 1-5, turn 300 (before -> after): techs 34.9 -> 38.5, civics 27.8 -> 31.8, era 3.8 -> 4.5, science 77 -> 103, culture 82 -> 137, cities 8.6 -> 10.5. The first 75 turns barely move: the opening is production-bound (one Settler about every 12-15 turns from the capital), close to Civ's own Prince pace; the later gap to Civ (roughly Industrial by turn 200) is the subject of milestones 2-3.
- Milestone 2 as built: `ai::strategies` / `ai::strategyName` and a per-turn `Posture` computed from the state (the AI keeps no memory, so it replays exactly). Victory strategies from the Classical era, at most one, by conditions met (Civ's lists, read against what the core has): Science 3 of (science 20% above the rivals' average, Renaissance or later, Campuses in half the cities, 15% more techs than the average, weaker army than the strongest rival); Culture 3 of (culture 20% above average, a Great Work, three Great Works; exclusive at 75% of the strongest rival's domestic tourists); Religious all 3 (faith 20% above average, a religion founded, two foreign cities not following it; disqualified when no religion can be founded); Domination 3 of (holds a rival's original capital, two or more rivals, strongest army, highest score, score 20% above the next; exclusive with two capitals held); Diplomatic 2 of (victory points, the most of them, a quarter of the target; exclusive at 60%). Situational: Rapid Expansion (free sites, no major war, not a Dark Age, under 16 cities), Naval (the capital's landmass under a fifth of the land, or Queen of the Seas), Wonder Obsessed (First Emperor), Dark Age. Effects: yield weights per era (Ancient food and production first, later science and culture) plus the strategy's yield; the victory strategy's district at 150-180%; Domination wants 60% more army and goes to war at 110% of the target's strength (130% normally; Dark Age +30); Rapid Expansion settlers +30%; Wonder Obsessed wonders x2; agendas add flavour (expansion, faith, army and war appetite, gold, culture, food). `sovsim --bench` prints how many civs hold each strategy per checkpoint. Benchmark at turn 300 vs milestone 1: population 87 -> 92, science 103 -> 121, techs 38.5 -> 39.5 (strategies spread: Science 7, Domination 4, Culture 2, Religious 1, Diplomatic 1 of 20 civs).
- Milestone 3 as built: the core gained unit upgrades (`Command::upgradeUnit`, `Game::upgradeCost` / `upgradeProblem`): in the owner's territory with moves left, the next unit in the line known and its strategic resource on hand; cost UPGRADE_BASE_COST + the production difference x UPGRADE_NET_PRODUCTION_PERCENT_COST, at least UPGRADE_MINIMUM_COST, scaled by speed (Warrior -> Swordsman 60); the unit keeps health, XP and promotions and spends its turn; the Unreal promotion chooser (U) offers it. AI: the operation against the target civ picks its nearest seen city, gathers at a staging plot 4 tiles out nearest our cities and assaults once the units within 6 tiles reach 2x the city's strength (3x and a siege unit against walls; AiOperationTeams' ratios); a war on walled cities builds one siege unit per four soldiers; units within 6 tiles reinforce a threatened city (about one per 10 threat); attacks finish the most wounded enemy first; upgrades bought by strength gained per gold above a reserve. Influence maps beyond the per-city threat count, naval superiority and escorted settlers wait for a later pass. Benchmark at turn 300: population 92 -> 79 and science 121 -> 113 (gold now goes to upgrades and wars run longer); techs 39.5 -> 38.5.
- Measure first: every economic change lands with its benchmark numbers in the PR. Targets are Sovereign's own (no published per-turn Civ VI curves): the Industrial era by about turn 200 and the end of the tech tree before turn 400 at Prince on Standard speed, without AI cheats (revised after milestone 1 showed the opening is production-bound and already near Civ's pace).
