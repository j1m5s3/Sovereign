# Plan: trained battle AI (step 6, item 3)

Status: done, 2026-10-05. Previous plans: `jit_history/` (city assaults went live in PR #32).

Goal (leader doc §10 layer 3, §11): real-time unit control trained by self-play in simulated battles, trained offline on our side, a small network that runs on any CPU. Civ math stays the backbone (§9): the core clamps every live result to the ±25% band, so the AI makes battles feel smart, it cannot flip the numbers.

## Decisions (Claude's recommendations, following the docs)

- **Headless battle library `battle/`** (plain C++17, no Unreal, no core rules). The live battle is a non-deterministic input to the core (engine doc, Core foundations), so it may use floats; it has its own seeded RNG so training and tests reproduce. Unreal compiles it inside the SovereignCore module through generated wrappers (the same tool as the core); `FSovBattleSim` becomes a thin adapter.
- **Tactics worth learning:** each unit's soldiers form three squads (left, centre, right). A commander gives each squad one of six orders every second: Advance, Hold (braced: harder to hit), Flank left, Flank right, Fall back, Hunt the leader. Blows from the flank or rear hit harder, and soldiers near their own leader fight better (the aura), so placement and timing matter; strength still decides the size of every blow.
- **The network:** a small MLP (about 40 inputs, 32 hidden, 18 outputs: six orders for each of three squads), observations mirrored so one network plays either side. Our own forward pass, behind a `Commander` interface; ONNX Runtime can replace it later without touching callers (engine doc: third-party runtimes behind small interfaces). The network is tiny, so a dependency would buy nothing yet.
- **Training:** evolution strategies (antithetic sampling, Adam) by self-play, in C++ with threads, no downloads. Opponents: the scripted baseline (everyone advances, today's behaviour), all-hold, and a pool of the network's earlier checkpoints. Fitness: enemy losses minus own losses over random matchups, plus a leader bonus.
- **Shipping:** weights in `data/battle_ai/commander.txt` (plain text, versioned header). Missing file: the scripted baseline, so nothing breaks.
- **In game:** the trained commander leads the side the human does not control. The human orders their own squads with keys 1 to 6 (Tab still toggles advance/hold) while fighting as the leader.
- **Difficulty:** a temperature on the policy (sampling instead of argmax) lowers its quality; wired as a parameter now, hooked to game difficulty when difficulty exists.

## Milestones

1. `battle/` library: sim with squads, orders, flanking, bracing, aura; observation and commander interface; scripted baseline; CMake target and tests (numbers decide most fights; flanking beats a frontal attack).
2. Trainer `battle_train` and the first trained weights; tests that the trained commander beats the baseline in even fights and does not flip clear mismatches.
3. Unreal: wrappers, `FSovBattleSim` adapter, enemy led by the trained commander, squad order keys and HUD; automation tests; docs.

## Outcome

- All three milestones built. `battle/` holds the simulation, the commander and the trainer; Unreal's `FSovBattleSim` is an adapter over it.
- **Tuning found on the way:**
  - Holders first defended only the spot they stood on, so their rear ranks never fought. They now defend a zone around an anchor.
  - Flankers marched straight into the enemy wing. They now go round its widest man.
  - Bonuses were raised to flank +6, rear +10, braced −5.
  - Even so, a greedy advance stays close to the best plan in even fights, which is realistic. The goal therefore became "hard to exploit".
- **Observations:** the enemy squads' movement was added (49 inputs). Without it the network could not tell an advancing enemy from one standing still.
- **Result:**
  - Training took 1,500 generations in about 7 minutes on 16 threads; the best version was kept.
  - Against the five scripted styles the shipped commander scores +0.16 to +0.67.
  - Its worst score (+0.16) beats every scripted commander's worst (−0.02 to −0.16).
  - It never upset a force 14 strength stronger in 56 tries.
- **Follow-ups:**
  - Difficulty levels to temperature, once game difficulty exists.
  - Nearby units joining a battle, which needs more than one unit per side.
  - Multiplayer: the absent human's units commanded by the battle AI.
  - Retraining when the simulation changes: `battle_train`.
