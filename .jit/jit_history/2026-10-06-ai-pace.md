# Plan: AI pace toward its targets (step 6, "everything else")

Status: done, 2026-10-06 (closed with findings; the gap stays open). Previous: `jit_history/2026-10-06-monopolies.md`. Chosen by Claude under James's standing consent: a full-length all-AI game (seed 11, 6 civs, Small, Standard speed) ends on Score at turn 500 with about 70 of 77 techs. The targets in `jit_history/2026-10-05-strategy-ai.md` are the Industrial era by about turn 200 and the end of the tech tree before turn 400. At turn 200 the AI is in era 2.4 (Medieval to Renaissance) with 24 techs.

## Method

Every change is measured with `sovsim --bench 8 --players 6 --size MAPSIZE_SMALL --turns 200`, against a build of main, and kept only if it gains. With 4 seeds the noise was about ±0.4 cities and ±5 science at turn 200.

Baseline (main after #118, 8 seeds), at turn 200: 7.6 cities, population 45.2, 24.4 techs, 18.0 civics, era 2.4, science 53.5, culture 38.4, production 86.3.

## Diagnosis (seed 1, turn 150, `diag` scratch tool)

- Tech costs and eureka share match Civ VI: Writing 50, Education 390; 5–11 eurekas out of about 20 techs.
- Cities average population 4.9 with a +4.8 food surplus. The capital has population 8 and is near its housing (9.5). Half the cities were founded after turn 80.
- Production samples: civilians 37%, military units about 30%, Libraries about 1%.
- Trade capacity is 1–2 per civ. Markets give the capacity, but Commercial Hubs are rarely built.

## Experiments

1. **Dropped** (8 seeds, turn 200: 7.4 cities, science 48.5 vs 7.6 and 53.5):
   - the AI scores promotions by their effects;
   - it values a Market's trade route;
   - it values a Commercial Hub while routes are few.
2. **Dropped:**
   - B, a Campus +120 in a city without one: 7.2 cities, 25.0 techs, science 56.2, culture 32.7. Cities and culture fall.
   - E, one more Settler in flight: 7.1 cities, 24.3 techs, science 51.0.
3. **Kept:** C, Science weighted 2 more in every era (`eraYields` in `assess`).
   - Alone: 7.6 cities, 25.3 techs, science 53.5.
   - With E: 7.8 cities, 25.4 techs, science 57.0.
   - About +1 tech by turn 200.

## Findings

- The core's rules behind pace match Civ VI: tech costs, growth threshold, base housing, science and culture per citizen, and the eureka share.
- The opening is reasonable: a Settler from the capital about every 11 turns, and capital production 9–10 by turn 25.
- What keeps the AI about 1.5 eras behind its own target:
  - secondary cities build military before settlers;
  - few Campuses and Libraries;
  - cities capped by housing.
- Weight tuning moves pace by a few percent at most. Closing the gap would need a planner: city specialisation (which city builds settlers, which builds Campuses), or a build-order opening. That would be its own plan.
- The target itself (Industrial era by turn 200) was set without published Civ VI curves; Civ VI's AI at Prince is commonly behind a human's pace.

## Follow-up: four more knobs (8 seeds, turn 200, on top of C: 7.6 cities, 25.3 techs, science 53.5)

- K1, Settlers worth 900 early (was 600): 7.5 cities, 24.7 techs, science 49.2.
- K2, peacetime army `n/2 + 2` (was `n + 1 + n/3`): 7.4 cities, 25.2 techs, science 56.9.
- K3, building base value 80 (was 30): 7.0 cities, 24.8 techs, science 53.2.
- K4, long-build span 16 turns (was 12): 7.7 cities, 25.0 techs, science 52.8.

None was kept: single-knob tuning sits at a local optimum.
