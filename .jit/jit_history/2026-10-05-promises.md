# Plan: promises (step 6, "everything else")

Status: done, 2026-10-05. Previous: `jit_history/2026-10-05-archaeology.md`. Chosen by Claude under James's standing consent: the Gathering Storm "Ask Promise" diplomatic actions from 08, which the core did not have. One milestone.

Specs: 08-diplomacy-government (Diplomatic actions, War types, Grievances); data/global-parameters (GRIEVANCE_MULTIPLIER_FOR_BROKEN_PROMISE).

## Milestone

1. **Done** (`GameState::promises`, `Promise`, `PromiseKind`, save version 52; command `AskPromise` 59; `CasusBelli::Retribution`): a major civ spends 30 Diplomatic Favor to ask another it has met, and is at peace with, to promise for 30 turns not to settle near it, spy on it, convert its cities or dig in its land. The other agrees when it does not dislike the asker (`wouldPromise`, the same opinion test as other deals); a refusal gives the asker 25 grievances against it. Breaking a promise (founding a city within 6 of the asker's cities, a spy operation against it, a Missionary or Apostle spreading in its cities, an excavation in its land) gives the asker GRIEVANCE_MULTIPLIER_FOR_BROKEN_PROMISE (200%) of a formal war's 100 grievances and a broken-deal memory. For 30 turns it also gives the asker a War of Retribution casus belli (Early Empire, after denouncing; 50% of a formal war's grievances). AI: with 60 favor it asks a neighbour whose cities sit within 6 of its own for the settling promise. Unreal: the diplomacy chooser (N) offers the four promises and names the new war type.

## Decisions (Claude's recommendations; James gave standing consent)

- The 30-turn length and 30-favor cost are Sovereign readings (Civ VI's promise timers are not in the extracted data).
- The AI keeps its promises only by not checking them: its settling, spying and missionary logic does not yet avoid promised civs, so AI civs can break promises (and suffer the grievances).
