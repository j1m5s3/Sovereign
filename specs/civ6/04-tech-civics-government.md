# 04 Technology, Civics, Government, Policies

## Two trees
- **Technology tree** progressed with Science. **Civics tree** progressed with Culture. Both are DAGs with prerequisites; each node has a cost (scaled by game speed), an era, unlocks (units, buildings, districts, improvements, wonders, policies, governments, abilities, resource reveals, projects), and a **boost** condition.
- One active research and one active civic at a time; may switch freely without losing progress (progress is stored per node).
- Overflow science/culture carries into the next node.
- **Boosts**: completing the boost condition grants 40% of the node cost (Eureka for techs, Inspiration for civics). Boosts can be earned before or during research. Some civs/policies change boost value (e.g., Babylon [GS]: Eurekas grant 100% of the tech's cost, but Babylon has −50% Science per turn). Free boosts come from tribal villages, Great People, spies (Steal Tech Boost), and city-state suzerain bonuses.
- Cost scaling: each node's base cost is set by its position/era and scaled by game speed. Costs do **not** scale with number of cities, and there is **no** discount for techs already known by civs you've met.
- Future Tech / Future Civic: repeatable, grants score and nothing else (GS).

## Governments
- Adopt a government after unlocking it (civic). There is no anarchy. Changing governments (and policies) is free on the turn any civic completes; at any other time it costs gold (scales with era; tunable).
- Government buildings are built in the Government Plaza [R&F]. Each government has an **Inherent bonus** (always active while adopted). In the base game a separate **Legacy bonus** accumulated over time; R&F removed legacy accumulation and folded those effects into each government's inherent bonuses (as in the table below).

## Policy cards
- Each civic unlocks some policy cards. Cards have a slot type: Military (red), Economic (yellow), Diplomatic (green), Great Person (purple). Wildcard slots accept any card; Great Person cards can only be slotted in Wildcard slots.
- Swapping policies is free on the turn a civic completes; otherwise costs gold (scales by era; tunable).
- Some cards become **obsolete** when later civics unlock replacements.
- Examples (data):
  - Military: Discipline (+5 vs barbarians), Survey (scouts double XP), Agoge (+50% production for Ancient/Classical melee & ranged), Maritime Industries (+100% naval prod), Maneuver (+50% cavalry prod), Conscription (−1 gold unit maintenance), Limitanei (+2 loyalty with garrison), Raid (+50% pillage yield), Professional Army (−50% upgrade cost), Retainers (+1 amenity for garrisoned cities), Levée en Masse, Lightning Warfare, Force Modernization, International Waters...

  - Diplomatic: Charismatic Leader (+2 influence points/turn), Diplomatic League (first envoy counts double), Merchant Confederation (+1 gold per envoy), Machiavellianism (spy bonuses), Wisselbanken, Containment, Raj, Arsenal of Democracy, Police State, Cryptography, Nuclear Espionage...

## Eras
- Eras: Ancient, Classical, Medieval, Renaissance, Industrial, Modern, Atomic, Information, Future [GS].
- **Player era (base)**: determined by the most advanced tech or civic era; affects AI agendas, warmonger penalties, grievances, era-based barbarians and tribal villages.
- **Global era [R&F]**: the world advances to the next era when either (a) a threshold share of surviving major civs (default: half; tunable) have entered the next era by tech/civic progress, or (b) the era has lasted its maximum turn count. Each era has min/max durations (scaled by speed). Global era change triggers age evaluation (see 09).
- Era-based effects: unit upgrade availability, warmonger penalty multipliers (Ancient none → escalates), increase in costs of districts, barbarian unit types, tribal village rewards.
