# 04 Technology, Civics, Government, Policies

Reference tables (generated from the game rules database): [technologies](data/technologies.md), [civics](data/civics.md), [governments and policies](data/governments-policies.md), [global parameters](data/global-parameters.md), [eras](data/eras-moments-loyalty.md). The lists below were checked against those tables; when they disagree, the data tables win.

## Two trees
- **Technology tree** progressed with Science. **Civics tree** progressed with Culture. Both are DAGs (`TechnologyPrereqs`, `CivicPrereqs`); each node has a cost (Standard speed, multiplied by the game speed's cost multiplier), an era, unlocks (units, buildings, districts, improvements, wonders, policies, governments, resource reveals, projects, modifiers such as envoys, spies and governor titles) and usually one **boost**.
- One active research and one active civic at a time; the player may switch freely without losing progress, since progress is stored per node (engine; unverified). Overflow Science/Culture carries into the next node (engine; unverified).
- **Boosts** (`Boosts` table): completing the boost condition grants `Boosts.Boost` percent of the node's cost: 40% for every boosted node except Near Future Governance (90%). Boosts can be earned before or during research. Babylon [DLC] raises the Eureka value by 100 percentage points (effectively the whole cost) at the price of −50% Science in all cities (data: `TraitModifiers`). Other boost sources: Great Library (all Ancient/Classical Eurekas), some Great Scientists' activations, tribal villages (one or two Eurekas/Inspirations), and the spy mission Steal Tech Boost (Nuclear Espionage adds one more).
- Late-game "critical" techs (Rocketry, Nuclear Fission, Telecommunications, Stealth Technology and all Future-era techs) have a boost row with no gameplay trigger (`BOOST_TRIGGER_NONE_LATE_GAME_CRITICAL_TECH`): they can only be boosted by Great Scientists, spies or other effects.
- **Cost scaling**: node base costs are fixed per node in data and scaled by game speed only; they do not scale with number of cities, and there is no discount for techs already known by civs you have met (engine; unverified). [GS] Nodes from eras earlier than the current world era cost 20% less and nodes from later eras cost 20% more (`TECH_COST_PERCENT_CHANGE_BEFORE_GAME_ERA = −20`, `..._AFTER_GAME_ERA = 20`, same for civics) (engine; source: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/rise-and-fall-ahead-of-time-tech-cost-increase.627265/).
- **Future era [GS]**: Future-era techs and civics have `RandomPrereqs` and `HiddenUntilPrereqComplete` set: each game the engine picks their prerequisites among late Information-era nodes, hides them until a prerequisite is done, and picks each cost from `TechnologyRandomCosts`/`CivicRandomCosts` (2200 or 2300 for most techs) (engine; unverified). Future Tech (2600) is repeatable and gives +5% production toward projects each time; Future Civic (3500) is repeatable and gives +50 Diplomatic Favor and a Governor title each time.

## Governments
- Governments are unlocked by civics (`Governments.PrereqCivic`). Chiefdom has no prerequisite; the first government and policies become available after the first civic is finished (engine; source: https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/govt_3/).
- Changing government or policies is free on any turn a new government or policy is unlocked from the civics tree; otherwise it costs Gold (engine; source: https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/govt_3/). The gold formula is not verified; `GlobalParameters` holds `POLICY_COST_BASE = 25`, `POLICY_COST_EXPONENT = 1.5`, `POLICY_COST_INCREASE_TO_BE_EXPONENTED = 3`, `POLICY_COST_VISIBLE_DIVISOR = 5`, `POLICY_ENACTED_DISCOUNT = 50`, whose exact use is unverified (engine; unverified).
- **Anarchy**: switching back to a government the civ has already used causes Anarchy: no Gold, Science, Culture or Faith, no government bonuses, no policies and no further government changes until it ends. It lasts at least 3 turns and longer the more often that government was used before (engine; source: https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/govt_5/). Data has `GOVERNMENT_BASE_ANARCHY_TURNS = 2`; how it maps to the documented minimum is unverified.
- Each government has two always-active bonuses (`GovernmentModifiers`). In the base game the second bonus grew over time ("legacy"); R&F removed the accumulation, and the second bonus is now flat while adopted. Tier 1–3 governments also unlock a Wildcard **legacy policy card** (`Governments.PolicyToUnlock`, e.g. Autocratic Legacy) that carries the inherent bonus after leaving that government; when the card becomes available is an engine rule (engine; unverified).
- Each government sets Influence Points per turn and the threshold for an Envoy (`InfluencePointsPerTurn`, `InfluencePointsThreshold`, `InfluenceTokensPerThreshold`) and [GS] Diplomatic Favor per turn (`Governments_XP2.Favor`). Tier 3 and 4 governments have `OtherGovernmentIntolerance = −20` (diplomatic penalty toward civs with a different government).
- Government buildings are built in the Government Plaza [R&F] (see file 03).

## Policy cards
- Each civic unlocks some policy cards (`Policies.PrereqCivic`). Slot types: Military, Economic, Diplomatic, Wildcard; Great Person cards have their own slot type but no government has Great Person slots, so in practice they go into Wildcard slots, which accept any card. Wonders add slots (Alhambra +1 Military, Big Ben +1 Economic, Potala Palace +1 Diplomatic, Forbidden City +1 Wildcard).
- Policies can be swapped for free on any turn a civic completes or a new slot is gained; otherwise swapping costs Gold (engine; source: https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/govt_3/; formula unverified, see Governments above).
- Many cards become **obsolete** when a later civic unlocks their replacement (`ObsoletePolicies`), e.g. Agoge → Feudal Contract → Grande Armée → Military First, God King → Scripture, Urban Planning → Rationalism, Natural Philosophy → Five-Year Plan, Town Charters and Naval Infrastructure → Economic Union, Aesthetics → Sports Media, Inspiration → Science Foundations, Revelation → Invention, Strategos → Military Organization, Serfdom → Public Works, Insulae → Medina Quarter → New Deal. The full chains are in [governments and policies](data/governments-policies.md).
- [GS] Some cards are exclusive to a government (`Policy_GovernmentExclusives_XP2`): Third Alternative and Martial Law (Fascism), Collectivization and Defense of the Motherland (Communism), New Deal and Their Finest Hour (Democracy).
- Examples (data):
  - Military: Discipline (+5 vs barbarians), Survey (+100% XP for recon), Agoge (+50% production toward Ancient/Classical melee, anti-cavalry and ranged), Maritime Industries (+100% toward Ancient/Classical naval), Maneuver (+50% toward Ancient/Classical cavalry), Conscription (−1 Gold unit maintenance), Limitanei (+2 Loyalty in garrisoned cities), Raid (+50% pillage yields), Professional Army (−50% upgrade cost), Retainers (+1 Amenity in garrisoned cities), Levée en Masse (−2 Gold unit maintenance), Lightning Warfare, Force Modernization, International Waters.

## Eras
- Eras: Ancient, Classical, Medieval, Renaissance, Industrial, Modern, Atomic, Information, Future [GS] (`Eras.ChronologyIndex` 1–9).
- **Player era**: each civ's own era follows the most advanced era of the techs and civics it has researched (engine; unverified). It drives Great Person base cost per era (`Eras.GreatPersonBaseCost`: 30, 60, 120, 240, 420, 660, 960, 1320, 1320), warmonger points (`Eras.WarmongerPoints`: 0 in Ancient, then 6, 12, 18, 24...), embarked unit strength and era-based barbarian units and tribal village rewards.
- **World (game) era [R&F]**: every era has a minimum and maximum length (`Eras_XP1.GameEraMinimumTurns = 40`, `GameEraMaximumTurns = 60` at Standard speed, scaled by game speed). After the minimum, a countdown of `NEXT_ERA_TURN_COUNTDOWN = 10` turns starts once enough civs have entered the next era (community analysis suggests about half of the civs; the first civ alone is not enough), or when the maximum is reached; when it runs out the world era advances (engine; source: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/era-speed-in-rise-and-fall.628319/). The exact threshold is unverified.
- A world era change triggers age evaluation (Golden/Normal/Dark, see 09) and changes era-scaled values: tech/civic cost ±20% relative to the world era [GS], grievance decay (`Eras_XP2.GrievanceDecayRate`, 10 down to 2), liberation envoys (`Eras_XP1.LiberatedEnvoys`) and a per-era trade route duration adjustment (`Eras_XP2.TradeRouteMinimumEndTurnChange`). District costs do not depend on era; they follow tree progress (file 03).
