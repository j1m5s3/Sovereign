# 09 Civilizations, Leaders, Ages, Victory, Climate

## Civilization and leader structure
Each **Civilization** has: name, city name list, start bias, **civ ability** (unique), **unique unit** (1, sometimes 2), **unique infrastructure** (district, building, or improvement), color palette.
Each **Leader** belongs to one civ (some civs have several leaders, and some leaders have alternate "personas" with different abilities), has: **leader ability**, sometimes a leader-specific unique unit/building/governor, **historical agenda**, AI personality weights (grand strategy preferences), voice/3D model. A leader may be playable for more than one civ (Eleanor of Aquitaine: England or France; Kublai Khan: China or Mongolia), with a separate leader entry per civ.

Data schema:
```
Civilization { id, name, adjectives, cities[], start_bias[{terrain|feature|resource|river|coast, tier}],
               ability_modifiers[], unique_units[], unique_infrastructure[] }
Leader { id, civ, name, ability_modifiers[], unique_units[], agenda, ai_weights{science, culture, religion,
         military, expansion, wonders, diplomacy, ...}, favored_civics/techs, favored_victory }
```

### Roster size
- This install (Gathering Storm ruleset with Rise and Fall content and all installed civ/leader packs) has **50 playable civilizations and 77 leader entries** (data: Civilizations with `CIVILIZATION_LEVEL_FULL_CIV`, CivilizationLeaders; persona and dual-civ leaders count once per entry).
- The complete roster with every civ trait, leader trait, start bias, unique and agenda (rendered from modifiers) is in [data/civilizations-leaders.md](data/civilizations-leaders.md).

## Era score and Ages [R&F]
Full tables: [data/eras-moments-loyalty.md](data/eras-moments-loyalty.md) (eras, every historic moment with its era score, dedications) and [data/governments-policies.md](data/governments-policies.md) (dark-age policies).
- **Historic Moments** add era score and are logged on a **Timeline** (with illustrations). Most come in a "first in world" and a "first for this player" variant, and many become obsolete in a later era (data: Moments.EraScore, ObsoleteEra, MinimumGameEra/MaximumGameEra). Representative values:
  - 5: world's first circumnavigation, world's first to meet all civs, world's first flight, first trading posts in all civs, final foreign city taken.
  - 4: world wonder completed (3 for a wonder from an earlier era), unique unit/building/district/improvement first used, foreign capital taken, rival Holy City converted, first discovery of a new continent (the world's first civ to see it; Hic Sunt Dracones adds +3 for every continent a civ sees), world's first satellite / moon landing / Mars colony / exoplanet launch.
  - 3: world's first religion, world's first natural wonder discovery, first fully developed district of a type ("Splendid ..."), threatening barbarian camp destroyed, enemy veteran defeated, met all civs, circumnavigation (not first), City of Awe (a city founded near a natural wonder; Sovereign counts within 2 tiles) (engine; unverified).
  - 2: religion founded, barbarian camp destroyed (Ancient–Medieval only), city on a new continent, world's first tech/civic of a new era, Great General/Admiral defeats an enemy, a city-state's first suzerain, world's first city of each size tier.
  - 1: meet a new civ, natural wonder discovered (not first), first tech/civic of a new era, Great Person recruited, trading post in a new civ, unit promoted with distinction, first city of each size tier, tribal village (Ancient only), pantheon.
- **Thresholds** per player, recomputed at each era change from GlobalParameters: `DARK_AGE_SCORE_BASE_THRESHOLD` = 14, `GOLDEN_AGE_SCORE_BASE_THRESHOLD` = 28, `THRESHOLD_SHIFT_PER_PAST_GOLDEN_AGE` = +5, `THRESHOLD_SHIFT_PER_PAST_DARK_AGE` = −5, plus the era's `Eras_XP2.EraScoreThresholdShift` (only Ancient has one: −3). Players also report +1 per city owned and observe 12 (dark) / 24 (golden) for a one-city Ancient start; how the engine combines these exactly is not in data (engine; source: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/the-golden-age-threshold-for-the-ancient-era-is-too-low.643450/). `THRESHOLD_SHIFT_PER_INCOMPLETE_ERA_TECH/CIVIC` exist but are 0.
- When the global era changes, each player's age for the new era is set:
  - Score < dark threshold → **Dark Age**: citizen loyalty pressure from the player's cities reduced by 0.5 per citizen (data: modifier `DARK_AGE_CITY_IDENTITY`); may slot **Dark Age policies** in Wildcard slots (data: Policies_XP1 RequiresDarkAge). The 13 dark-age policies, each with an era window: Monasticism, Twilight Valor, Inquisition, Isolationism (Classical start); Letters of Marque (Renaissance); Robber Barons, Elite Forces (Industrial); Collectivism (Modern); Rogue State, Flower Power (Atomic); Cyber Warfare, Automated Workforce, Disinformation Campaign (Information). Each pairs a strong bonus with a penalty (e.g., Monasticism +75% Science in Holy Site cities, −25% Culture). Past dark ages lower future thresholds (−5 each).
  - Between → **Normal Age**.
  - ≥ golden threshold → **Golden Age**: citizen loyalty pressure +0.5 per citizen (data: `GOLDEN_AGE_CITY_IDENTITY`); the dedication's golden bonus applies; future thresholds +5.
  - Golden threshold reached directly after a Dark Age → **Heroic Age**: a Golden Age in which you choose 3 dedications (data: `COMMEMORATE_OPTIONS_MAX` = 3; normal is `COMMEMORATE_BASE_CHOICES_ALLOWED` = 1).
- **Dedications** (commemorations) chosen at each new era (1 normally, 3 in a Heroic Age); each is available only in an era window (data: CommemorationTypes). In a Normal or Dark Age a dedication awards era score for specific actions; in a Golden Age it gives a bonus instead. Full list (era window: normal-age goal / golden bonus):
  - Free Inquiry (Classical–Medieval): +1 per Eureka and per science building / Eurekas +10%, Commercial Hub and Harbor also yield Science equal to their Gold adjacency.
  - Pen, Brush and Voice (Classical–Medieval): +1 per Inspiration and per culture building / Inspirations +10%, +1 Culture per district.
  - Monumentality (Classical–Renaissance): +1 per district built / civilian land units purchasable with Faith, Builders and Settlers 30% cheaper to buy, Builders +2 Movement.
  - Exodus of the Evangelists (Classical–Renaissance): +2 per city converted / religious units +2 Movement and +2 spreads, +4 Great Prophet points per turn.
  - Hic Sunt Dracones (Renaissance–Modern): +3 per new continent or natural wonder found, +1 per naval kill / naval and embarked units +2 Movement, cities founded off the home continent start with +3 Population, +2 Loyalty per turn in those cities.
  - Reform the Coinage (Renaissance–Modern): +1 per trade route completed / international routes +3 Gold per specialty district at the destination, routes immune to plunder.
  - Heartbeat of Steam (Industrial–Atomic): +1 per Industrial Zone building / +10% Production toward Industrial-and-later wonders, Campuses yield Production equal to their Science adjacency.
  - To Arms! (Industrial–Atomic): +1 per corps killed, +2 per army killed / +15% Production toward military units.
  - Wish You Were Here (Atomic+): +1 per artifact extracted / +100% National Park tourism, +50% tourism in cities with a governor.
  - Bodyguard of Lies (Atomic+): +1 per successful spy mission / Spies establish 100% faster and run offensive ops 25% faster.
  - Sky and Stars (Information+): +1 per Aerodrome building and per Great Person / free Eurekas for late space techs, +100% XP for air units, +2 Aluminum accumulation per source.
  - Automaton Warfare (Information+): +1 per kill by a Giant Death Robot / one free GDR in the capital, extra Uranium per turn and per source.
- **Global era transitions**: each era lasts between 40 and 60 turns on Standard speed (data: Eras_XP1.GameEraMinimumTurns/MaximumTurns; Future Era has no limit) and players get a 10-turn warning (data: `NEXT_ERA_TURN_COUNTDOWN`). What triggers the advance between those bounds (how many civs have reached the next era) is engine (engine; unverified). The new era also changes grievance decay (10/turn in Ancient down to 2 in Future, data: Eras_XP2.GrievanceDecayRate), minimum trade route duration (data: Eras_XP2.TradeRouteMinimumEndTurnChange) and Great Person base cost (data: Eras.GreatPersonBaseCost).

## Victory conditions (each can be toggled at setup)
Data: Victories table (all enabled by default); scoring and turn limits in [data/game-setup.md](data/game-setup.md); space projects in [data/projects.md](data/projects.md).
1. **Science** [GS] (data: `VICTORY_TECHNOLOGY`, requirement set `REQUIREMENTS_EXOPLANET_TECHNOLOGY_VICTORY`): one-off projects in a Spaceport, each requiring the previous one:
   - Launch Earth Satellite (Rocketry, 900 Production; reveals the whole map) → Launch Moon Landing (Satellites, 1500; one-time Culture equal to 10× current Science per turn) → Launch Mars Colony (Nanotechnology, 1800; a single project in GS) → Launch Exoplanet Expedition (Smart Materials, 2100).
   - The expedition must travel **50 light-years** (data: `SCIENCE_VICTORY_POINTS_REQUIRED` = 50). Completing the expedition project gives a speed of 1 ly/turn. Speed sources (all add to the expedition's ly/turn): Build Terrestrial Laser Station (Offworld Mission, 600 Production, +1 ly/turn, only counts if the city is powered, and raises that city's power need by 5), Launch Lagrange Laser Station (Offworld Mission, 600 Production plus 30 Aluminum, +1 ly/turn), both repeatable; first place in the International Space Station competition (+3 ly/turn). Great People such as Wernher von Braun and Robert Goddard, the Integrated Space Cell policy and the Educator governor speed up space project Production rather than the expedition.
   - Win when the expedition arrives. The Spaceport city can be captured, which halts further projects there (engine; unverified).
   - Base game (no expansion, not in this ruleset's data): Earth Satellite, Moon Landing and three Mars modules (Reactor, Habitation, Hydroponics), win when all are done.
2. **Culture**: your visiting tourists exceed every other civ's domestic tourists (engine; source: https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Victory_(Civ6)). Tourism mechanics in 07.
3. **Domination**: hold the original capital of every other major civ; city-states don't count (engine; source: https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Victory_(Civ6)).
4. **Religious**: your religion is predominant (followed by more than half the cities) in every major civ (engine; source: https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Victory_(Civ6)). Details in 06.
5. **Diplomatic** [GS]: 20 diplomatic victory points (data: `DIPLOMATIC_VICTORY_POINTS_REQUIRED`). Sources in 08.
6. **Score**: highest score when the calendar runs out. The turn limit is the sum of GameSpeed_Turns per speed: Online 250, Quick 330, Standard 500, Epic 750, Marathon 1500. Score line items (data: ScoringLineItems): 3 per civic and 2 per tech (cost-scaled), 5 per city, 2 per district, 1 per Population, 5 per Great Person, 5 for a founded religion, 15 per wonder (cost-scaled), plus era-score, converted-city and era-building items.
- **Default victory**: the last remaining major civ wins (data: `VICTORY_DEFAULT`). Players are **eliminated** when they lose all cities (engine; unverified).

## Climate and Disasters [GS]
Full tables (event frequencies, damage percentages, fertility, coastal lowlands, deforestation): [data/climate-disasters.md](data/climate-disasters.md).
- **Global CO2**: burning a resource for power adds CO2 per unit burned: Coal 820, Oil 490, Uranium 48 (data: Resource_Consumption.CO2perkWh). Units that consume Coal/Oil/Uranium as maintenance add CO2 at 50% of that rate (data: `CLIMATE_CO2_PERCENT_FROM_UNITS` = 50). Each civ's share of total emissions costs Diplomatic Favor (data: `FAVOR_CO2_DIVISOR` = 3, between 0 and −20 per turn).
- **Temperature**: the global temperature rises 1 °C for every `Maps_XP2.CO2For1DegreeTempRise` of accumulated CO2 (Duel 500,000; Tiny 1,000,000; Small 1,500,000; Standard 2,000,000; Large 2,500,000; Huge 3,000,000). Deforestation scales this: the world's average deforestation level changes CO2's effect from −20% (little loss) to +50% (heavy loss) (data: DeforestationEffects).
- **Climate Phases I–VII**: each phase needs a number of climate change points: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 (data: RandomEvents.ClimateChangePoints for the `SEA_LEVEL_RISE1..7` events). One point equals a 0.5 °C rise; Disaster Intensity can add points (all 0 in the shipped RealismSettings) (engine; source: https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Climate_(Civ6)).
- Effects per phase (data: RandomEvents for the climate events, CoastalLowlands):

| Phase | Sea level | Polar ice lost | Coastal lowlands | Fertility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | +0.5 m | 10% | — | normal |
| II | +1.0 m | 20% | 1 m tiles flood | normal |
| III | +1.5 m | 30% | 2 m tiles flood | normal |
| IV | +2.0 m | 40% | 1 m tiles submerged | storms/floods stop adding fertility |
| V | +2.5 m | 55% | 3 m tiles flood | storms/droughts remove earlier fertility (15% chance) |
| VI | +3.0 m | 70% | 2 m tiles submerged | removal chance 30% |
| VII | +3.5 m | 85% | 3 m tiles submerged | removal chance 45% |

  - Coastal Lowlands are flagged at map generation (1/2/3 m bands; `CLIMATE_CHANGE_PERCENT_COASTAL_LOWLANDS` = 45). Flooded tiles lose improvements and yields until repaired; submerged tiles become permanent water. A city protects its lowlands with the **Flood Barrier** building (Computers; base cost 80, multiplied by the number of lowland tiles and by sea level already reached, data: Buildings_XP2.CostMultiplierPerTile/PerSeaLevel).
  - Higher temperature raises disaster odds: floods +20% per degree, the severe storm/drought variants +50% per degree (data: RandomEvents.ChanceIncreasePerDegree).
- Mitigation: renewable power (Solar Farm, Wind Farm, Offshore Wind Farm, Geothermal Plant, Hydroelectric Dam), Carbon Recapture project (Industrial Zone, Global Warming Mitigation civic, 400 Production; removes 50,000 CO2 and grants 30 Favor), decommissioning fossil plants, World Congress resolutions.
- **Natural disasters**: random per turn, starting turn 2 (data: `RANDOM_EVENT_START_TURN`); each event has an expected number of occurrences per game per **Disaster Intensity** setting (Minimal, Light, Moderate, Heavy, Hyperreal; data: RandomEvent_Frequencies), and the setting also sets the share of volcanoes that are active (45/60/70/80/95%) and, on Heavy/Hyperreal, +1 range (data: RealismSettings). Example (Moderate): Moderate Flood 2, Major Flood 1.5, 1000 Year Flood 1, Major Drought 23, Withering Drought 5, Tornado Family 15, Tornado Outbreak 3, Category-4 Hurricane 15, Category-5 Hurricane 3.
  - **Volcanic eruption** (Gentle / Catastrophic / Megacolossal; active volcanoes and volcano natural wonders): pillages improvements and buildings, the stronger ones destroy improvements, kill Population and civilians and damage units (Megacolossal 60–90 HP); afterwards **Volcanic Soil** with a chance of +1 Food (25–75%), +1 Production (15–35%), +1 Science (Catastrophic and up) or +1 Culture (some natural-wonder eruptions). The volcano natural wonders are always active and have their own eruption rows, each with its own frequency, damage and fertility (data: the RandomEvents rows naming a NaturalWonder): Mount Kilimanjaro Gentle or Catastrophic, Mount Vesuvius Megacolossal (all of a neighbouring city's population but 1, +1 Culture soil), Eyjafjallajökull Catastrophic or Megacolossal. In the data's later tables an event name's rows come in one run per event row, in the same order (natural-wonder variant, ordinary volcano, Eyjafjallajökull).
  - **River flood** (Moderate / Major / 1000 Year, on floodplains): pillages improvements and buildings; Major and 1000 Year also kill Population (15% / 25%) and damage units; afterwards a chance of +1 Food / +1 Production per tile. Dams on the river (and the Great Bath wonder) prevent floods; Egypt is immune.
  - **Drought** (Major 5 turns / Withering 10 turns, 7 tiles): −1 Food per affected tile and pillages farms (data: `DROUGHT_FOOD_LOSS_PER_TILE`). Aqueducts and Baths prevent it.
  - **Storms** (moving, 3 turns, spaced at least 15 tiles apart): Blizzards (Significant 7 tiles / Crippling 19), Dust Storms (Gradient 3 / Haboob 7), Tornadoes (Family 1 / Outbreak 3), Hurricanes (Category 4: 7 / Category 5: 19; both also pillage coastal lowland tiles). Strong variants damage units and kill Population; most leave fertility (+1 Food, some +1 Production).
  - **Forest and jungle fires**: burn Woods/Rainforest (9 turns), spread with 50% chance, kill civilians and damage units, then regrow with +1 yield.
  - **Meteor shower**: pillages tiles in one hex.
  - **Nuclear accident** at Nuclear Power Plants (Radioactive Steam Venting, Major Radiation Leak, Nuclear Meltdown): risk begins 10, 20 and 30 turns after the reactor was built or recommissioned (data: RandomEvents.MinTurnAtRisk), with fallout lasting 2, 10 and 20 turns; the Recommission Nuclear Reactor project (400 Production) resets the reactor's age.
- Disasters can open World Congress aid-request emergencies (Disaster Relief); the top contributor earns a diplomatic victory point (engine; source: https://www.well-of-souls.com/civ/civ6_gatheringstorm.html). See 08 and [data/world-congress-emergencies.md](data/world-congress-emergencies.md).

## Power [GS]
- Buildings that need Power (data: Buildings_XP2.RequiredPower): Factory 2, Electronics Factory 2, Stadium 2, Aquatics Center 2, Food Market 1, Shopping Mall 1, Airport 1, Stock Exchange 3, Research Lab 3, Broadcast Center 3, Film Studio 3. Seaports and Spaceports need none, though each Terrestrial Laser Station adds 5 to its city's requirement.
- A fully powered building gains a fixed bonus (data: Building_YieldChangesBonusWithPower): Factory +3 Production, Electronics Factory +5 Production, Stock Exchange +7 Gold, Research Lab +5 Science, Broadcast Center and Film Studio +4 Culture, Food Market +2 Food, Shopping Mall +2 Gold, Airport +2 Production; Stadium, Aquatics Center and Shopping Mall also give extra Amenities from entertainment when powered. A city short of power also suffers a Production penalty of up to −50% (data: `POWER_MAX_PRODUCTION_MODIFIER_PENALTY`).
- Power sources:
  - Power plants in the Industrial Zone burn 1 resource per turn: Coal Power Plant 4 Power per Coal, Oil Power Plant 4 per Oil, Nuclear Power Plant 16 per Uranium (data: Resource_Consumption.PowerProvided). They power every city whose center is within 6 tiles (data: Buildings.RegionalRange = 6). The Industrial Zone can convert between plant types or decommission them with projects (Convert to Coal 200, Oil 300, Nuclear 400, each Decommission 400 Production).
  - Local, no CO2: Hydroelectric Dam +6, Geothermal Plant +4, Solar Farm +2, Wind Farm +2, Offshore Wind Farm +2 (each renewable +2 more with Reyna's Renewable Subsidizer promotion); Cardiff city-state suzerain +2 per Lighthouse/Shipyard/Seaport; Synthetic Technocracy government +3 per city.
- Which resource a plant burns is fixed by the plant type; the city burns as many units as it needs to meet its power demand, up to its stockpile (engine; unverified).
