# 07 Economy, Trade, Great People, Great Works, Tourism

Reference tables: [data/districts.md](data/districts.md) (trade-route yields per district), [data/buildings.md](data/buildings.md) (Great Work slots and theming), [data/great-people.md](data/great-people.md) (classes, era costs, every individual, Great Work yields), [data/wonders.md](data/wonders.md), [data/improvements.md](data/improvements.md), [data/governments-policies.md](data/governments-policies.md), [data/global-parameters.md](data/global-parameters.md).

## Gold
- Empire treasury. Income: tiles, Commercial Hubs/Harbors and their buildings, trade routes, Palace, city-states, policies, specialists, plunder, selling/trading with other civs, deals (gold per turn).
- Expenses (per turn): building maintenance (non-wonder buildings 0–3 G; data: Buildings.Maintenance), district maintenance (0 or 1 G per district; data: Districts.Maintenance), unit maintenance set per unit type (data: Units.Maintenance; e.g. Warrior 0, Swordsman 2, Knight/Musketman 4, Infantry/Tank 6, Modern Armor 8, Giant Death Robot 15; civilians such as Settlers, Builders and Traders 0); free units from government/policies; deals paid per turn. Governors have no maintenance.
- Treasury below 0 [Base]: each full 10 gold of debt costs −1 Amenity in every city and disbands one unit; at −20 two units, and so on (data: GlobalParameters.GOLD_NEGATIVE_BALANCE_* = 0 / −10 / −10 / −10; engine behaviour per source: https://civ6.fandom.com/wiki/Amenities).
- Gold purchase: units/buildings/tiles; Great People via Patronage; unit upgrades; levying city-state militaries; diplomatic favor via deals [GS]; policy swaps outside a civic completion [GS]. Envoys cannot be bought with gold.

## Trade routes
- **Capacity** sources (data: modifiers with effect ADJUST_TRADE_ROUTE_CAPACITY):
  - Foreign Trade civic +1.
  - [R&F/GS] Each **Market** +1 (Sukiennice counts as a Market); each **Lighthouse** +1 only if that city has no Market. So at most +1 per city from these buildings. (The base-game rule "+1 per Commercial Hub district" is replaced by the building rule.)
  - Wonders: Colossus +1 (also grants a Trader), Great Zimbabwe +1.
  - Great People (one-time): Zhang Qian, Marco Polo (+free Trader), Ibn Fadlan, Melitta Bentz (Great Merchants), Zheng He (Great Admiral, +free Trader), Antonio Nariño (Comandante General, Gran Colombia only).
  - Civ/leader abilities: Elizabeth I +2 (while she has a Great Admiral), Persia +1 with Political Philosophy, Dido +1 per Government Plaza and per government building in the city, Victoria +1 per city founded on a foreign continent, Mansa Musa +1 when entering a Golden Age, João III +1 per civ met, Cree +1 (and a free Trader) with Pottery.
  - World Congress Trade Policy resolution [GS], option A: +1 capacity for the target player.
  - Merchant Republic gives no capacity in GS (its R&F/GS inherent bonus is +10% Gold in governed cities; the base-game +2 routes is gone).
- **Trader** unit (unlocked by the Foreign Trade civic): base cost 40 Production, rising with game progress (data: Units.CostProgressionModel = COST_PROGRESSION_GAME_PROGRESS, param 400; exact curve is engine; unverified). Purchasable with gold. 2 Movement, no maintenance, cannot fight.
- **Range**: 15 tiles over land, 30 over water (data: TRADE_ROUTE_BASE_RANGE = 15, TRADE_ROUTE_LAND_RANGE_REFUEL = 15, TRADE_ROUTE_WATER_RANGE_REFUEL = 30). Range is measured from the last city holding one of your Trading Posts, so a chain of posts lets routes reach further. Traders can cross water once embarkation is available (Celestial Navigation or Shipbuilding); sea routes need maritime access at both ends (coastal city, Harbor, or a Canal [GS]) (engine; source: https://forums.civfanatics.com/resources/trade-routes-guide.25529/). Routes on land lay **roads** along their path (data: TRADE_ROUTE_PLACES_ROADS = 1); Persia's trait upgrades those roads one level.
- **Duration**: the Trader shuttles between origin and destination; the route ends when it gets back to the origin after the minimum duration has passed. Minimum = 20 turns on Standard speed (data: TRADE_ROUTE_TURN_DURATION_BASE) plus a world-era increment [GS] (data: Eras_XP2.TradeRouteMinimumEndTurnChange: +0 Ancient/Classical, +10 Medieval/Renaissance, +20 Industrial/Modern/Atomic, +30 Information/Future). In practice the duration is the smallest whole number of round trips (2 × path length each) that exceeds that minimum (engine; source: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/trade-route-length-formula.648856/). Yields flow every turn from establishment. When the route ends the Trader is free to pick a new route (or repeat the old one).
- **Yields**. All base yields come from District_TradeRouteYields, applied per district present in the **destination** city and paid to the **origin** city (every YieldChangeAsOrigin value is 0). Summary of [data/districts.md](data/districts.md):

  | Destination district | Domestic route (to origin) | International route (to origin) |
  |---|---|---|
  | City Center | +1 Food, +1 Production | +3 Gold |
  | Campus / Holy Site / Theater Square (and their unique replacements: Observatory, Seowon, Lavra, Acropolis) | +1 Food | +1 Science / +1 Faith / +1 Culture |
  | Commercial Hub, Harbor (and Cothon, Suguba, Royal Navy Dockyard) | +1 Production | +3 Gold |
  | Industrial Zone, Encampment (and Hansa, Oppidum, Ikanda, Thành) | +1 Production | +1 Production |
  | Entertainment Complex, Water Park (and Street Carnival, Copacabana, Hippodrome) | +1 Food | +1 Food |
  | Government Plaza | +1 Food, +1 Production | +2 Gold |
  | Diplomatic Quarter | +1 Food, +1 Production | +1 Culture |

  - **Domestic route**: only the origin gains (Food/Production per the table); the destination gets nothing by default.
  - **International route** (to another major civ): the origin gains per the table; the destination gets nothing by default. Policies and abilities add more (e.g. Caravansaries +2 Gold, Triangular Trade +4 Gold +1 Faith, Trade Confederation +1 Culture +1 Science on international routes, Market Economy +2 Culture +2 Science and +1 Gold per luxury/strategic at the destination, Ecommerce +5 Gold +2 Production). There is no automatic bonus for luxuries at the destination [GS] (Resource_TradeRouteYields is empty).
  - Alliance routes [R&F]: level 1 alliances add yields both ways (Economic +4 Gold origin / +2 destination; Research, Cultural and Religious +2 Science/Culture/Faith origin and +1 destination). Democracy adds +4 Food and +4 Production at both ends of routes to allies and to city-states it is suzerain of.
  - **City-state route**: yields as international, plus a type bonus to the sender (data: MINOR_CIV_*_SEND_TRADE_ROUTE_BONUS: Scientific +1 Science, Cultural +1 Culture, Religious +1 Faith, Industrial/Militaristic +1 Production, Trade +2 Gold). Quests may ask for one.
- Every route also: grants diplomatic visibility of the destination civ (Trader visibility source), raises the origin owner's tourism toward the destination civ by +25% (data: TOURISM_TRADE_ROUTE_BONUS), and spreads religious pressure (data: RELIGION_SPREAD_TRADE_ROUTE_PRESSURE_FOR_DESTINATION 1.0, _FOR_ORIGIN 0.5; see 06).
- **Trading Posts**: created when a route to or from a city completes for the first time, owned by the route's owner; your own cities effectively count too. Effects: range refuels in any city with your post, and each of your posts in a **foreign** city adds +1 Gold to every route of yours that passes through it (data: TRADING_POST_GOLD_IN_FOREIGN_CITY = 1, _IN_OWN_CITY = 0) (engine; source: https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/trade_4/).
- Plunder: an enemy military unit entering the Trader's tile destroys it and receives a gold payout (amount engine; unverified). Naval raiders and some abilities increase plunder yields.

## Diplomatic deals (trade screen)
Deal item categories in data (DealItems): gold (lump sum, or per turn for 30 turns), resources (luxury and strategic, per turn), cities (mainly peace deals), agreements (open borders, delegation/embassy, joint war, alliances [R&F], research agreement), Great Works, captives (spies), units, technology [schema only; not offered in standard rules (engine; unverified)], and diplomatic favor [GS]. Each item valued by AI using utility (see AI file).

## Great People
Classes (data: GreatPersonClasses): Great General, Great Admiral, Great Engineer, Great Merchant, Great Prophet (max 1 per player), Great Scientist, Great Writer, Great Artist, Great Musician. Comandante General is a Gran Colombia–only General variant (not in the shared timeline; duplicates allowed).
- Each class has a roster of historical individuals tagged with an era (full list in [data/great-people.md](data/great-people.md)). Earliest eras: Classical for Generals, Admirals, Merchants, Scientists, Writers and Prophets (Prophets end at Renaissance); Medieval for Engineers; Renaissance for Artists; Industrial for Musicians. At any time each class exposes **one** current individual to all players. The GP era tracks the world era (the era most civs have entered); when the world era moves on, older unclaimed individuals drop out (engine; source: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/the-mechanism-of-great-people.619356/; exact selection rule unverified).
- **GPP**: per class, per player, accumulated each turn from districts (+1 of the matching class; Theater Square gives +1 each of Writer/Artist/Musician; Lavra and Royal Navy Dockyard +2), buildings (+1 to +3, e.g. Library/University/Research Lab +1 Scientist, Art Museum +2 Artist +1 Writer, Broadcast Center +2 Musician +1 Artist), wonders, policy cards (e.g. Inspiration +2 Scientist; Symphonies +4 Musician), projects, governors (Pingala's Grants +100% in the city), Classical Republic +15%.
- **Cost**: base cost by the individual's era (data: Eras.GreatPersonBaseCost: Ancient 30, Classical 60, Medieval 120, Renaissance 240, Industrial 420, Modern 660, Atomic 960, Information 1320, Future 1320), scaled by game speed. In R&F/GS cost no longer rises per person recruited (the base game used base + 60 per previous person). Individuals whose era is ahead of the world era cost a premium (about +30% per era ahead) (engine; source: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/the-mechanism-of-great-people.619356/; exact curve unverified).
- **Recruit**: when a player's points in a class reach the current individual's cost, that player recruits them and the cost is deducted (other players keep their points).
- **Patronage**: buy the current individual for gold = 200 + 15 × missing points, or faith = 150 + 10 × missing points; the fixed 200/150 part does not scale with game speed (engine; source: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/formula-thread.600534/; formula from vanilla analysis, unverified for GS). Others' progress unaffected.
- **Pass**: decline the current individual at no cost; points are kept, the individual stays available to everyone, and this player is not prompted again for that individual.
- Usage: each individual has charges and an activation (often only on a specific completed district, see the "Activation requirements" column), creates Great Works, or (Generals/Admirals) gives a passive aura plus a one-charge "retire" effect. Examples (all from data):
  - Great Scientist: Euclid (Eureka for Mathematics + 1 random Medieval Eureka), Hypatia (+1 Science to all Libraries; grants a Library), Isaac Newton (grants Library and University; +2 Science on all Universities), Albert Einstein (1 random Modern/Atomic Eureka; +4 Science on all Research Labs).
  - Great Engineer: adds Production to a wonder under construction (Isidore 215, Brunelleschi 315, Eiffel 480; Imhotep 350 for Ancient/Classical wonders, 175 otherwise; all × game speed), or other effects (Leonardo da Vinci: random Modern Eureka, +3 Culture on Workshops; James Watt: grants Workshop and Factory, +2 Production on Factories; Ada Lovelace/Bi Sheng: +1 district capacity).
  - Great Merchant: Zhang Qian / Marco Polo (+1 route capacity; Polo also a Trader), Colaeus (100 Faith + copy of a luxury), Adam Smith (500 Gold + 1 Governor Title), John Rockefeller (+3 Oil per turn; route gold per strategic at destination), Irene of Athens (+1 Governor Title), artificial-luxury creators (John Spilsbury Toys, Helena Rubinstein Cosmetics, Levi Strauss Jeans, Estée Lauder Perfume).
  - Great Prophet: found religion (see 06).
  - Great Writer/Artist/Musician: create Great Works in a free matching slot (Writers 2 Writing each; Artists 3 art works of a single type each; Musicians 2 Music each, 3 for Dimitrie Cantemir and Scott Joplin). Great Musicians do not perform concerts; concert tourism bursts are the Rock Band's role.
  - Great General/Admiral: aura +5 Combat Strength and +1 Movement for land (sea) units within 2 tiles whose era is the GP's era or the next; one retire effect.

## Great Works and Culture
- Object types (data: GreatWorkObjectTypes): Writing, Sculpture, Portrait, Landscape, Religious (the four "art" types), Music, Relic, Artifact.
- Slots (data: Building_GreatWorks; Libraries have none): Palace 1 (any type), Amphitheater 2 Writing, Art Museum 3 Art, Archaeological Museum 3 Artifact, Broadcast Center 1 Music, Temple 1 Relic, Cathedral 1 Religious art, plus wonders (Great Library 2 Writing, Oxford University 2 Writing, Hermitage 4 Art, Mont St. Michel 2 Relic, St. Basil's Cathedral 3 Relic, Bolshoi Theatre 1 Writing + 1 Music, Broadway 1 Writing + 2 Music, Sydney Opera House 3 Music), government buildings [R&F] (Queen's Bibliotheque 2 Writing/2 Art/2 Music; National History Museum 4 any) and unique buildings (Stave Church, Prasat 1 Relic; Film Studio 1 Music; Apadana 2 any). Full table in [data/buildings.md](data/buildings.md).
- Yields per work (data: GreatWork_YieldChanges and GreatWorks.Tourism; counts in [data/great-people.md](data/great-people.md)):

  | Type | Yield | Tourism |
  |---|---|---|
  | Writing | +2 Culture (8 works give +4) | 2 (4 for the +4 works) |
  | Art (Sculpture/Portrait/Landscape/Religious) | +3 Culture | 2 (9 Portrait/Landscape works give 4) |
  | Music | +4 Culture | 4 |
  | Artifact | +3 Culture | 3 |
  | Relic | +4 Faith | 8 |

  Great Works produce tourism from the moment they are placed (no tech needed). Policies, Pingala's Curator and Mary Leakey scale this tourism (e.g. Heritage Tourism ×2 for art and artifacts, Satellite Broadcasts ×3 for music).
- **Theming bonuses** (only two buildings in GS data): **Art Museum** – all 3 works the same object type, each by a different artist; **Archaeological Museum** – all 3 artifacts from the same era, each from a different civilization. A themed building gets +100% Culture and +100% Tourism from its works (data: ThemingYieldMultiplier = ThemingTourismMultiplier = 100).
- A newly moved/traded art work is locked for 10 turns (data: GREATWORK_ART_LOCK_TIME; engine meaning unverified).
- **Archaeology**: after Natural History, Antiquity Sites (land, up to 6 per civ) and Shipwrecks [R&F] (sea, up to 2 per civ) are placed on tiles of past battles and similar history up to the Renaissance (data: ARCHAEOLOGY_SITES_PER_CIV_LAND/SEA, ARCHAEOLOGY_MAX_ERA). The Archaeologist (Natural History; 400 Production, gold-purchasable; needs an Archaeological Museum in the city, one Archaeologist per museum, data: Unit_BuildingPrereqs) excavates a site for an Artifact or creates a **Landmark** improvement (tourism). Digging in foreign territory needs open borders and can break a promise.
- **National Parks**: the Naturalist (Conservation civic; faith only, 300 Faith +50 per previous copy) designates a 4-tile diamond of Charming-or-better appeal tiles owned by one city, with no districts or most improvements; the park yields tourism equal to the tiles' appeal, and gives Amenities to its city and nearby cities (data: NATIONAL_PARK_AMENITIES_OWNING_CITY = 2, NATIONAL_PARK_NUM_OTHER_AMENITY_CITIES = 4).
- **Seaside Resorts, Ski Resorts**: tourism = tile appeal (data: Improvement_Tourism source APPEAL, no tech needed).
- **Rock Bands** [GS]: unlocked by Cold War, faith only (300 Faith +50 per previous copy); perform a concert in a foreign city for a tourism burst; up to 4 promotions/levels (data: ROCK_BAND_MAX_LEVEL); each concert can end with the band disbanding (outcome table: Unit_RockbandResults_XP2).

## Tourism and Culture Victory
- **Domestic tourists** (per player) = (lifetime culture generated, including culture from Inspirations) / 100 (data: TOURISM_CULTURE_PER_CITIZEN = 100; source: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/formula-thread.600534/).
- **Visiting tourists** to you from civ X = floor(lifetime tourism you have generated toward X / (200 × number of major civs at game start)) (data: TOURISM_TOURISM_TO_MOVE_CITIZEN = 200; scaling by civ count is engine; source: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/questions-about-the-tourism-tab.675163/). The divisor does not shrink when civs are eliminated. Each visiting tourist you draw from X is also subtracted from X's domestic tourists (engine; same source). Your total visiting tourists = sum over all other civs.
- Tourism modifiers toward X: +25% if X has open borders with you (TOURISM_OPEN_BORDERS_BONUS), +25% if you have a trade route to X (TOURISM_TRADE_ROUTE_BONUS), Online Communities +50% toward civs you have a route to, Sarah Breedlove and Melitta Bentz +25% each toward those civs, Cultural alliance level 3 shares tourism, wonders and civ abilities; Synthetic Technocracy −10%.
- **Culture victory**: you win when your total visiting tourists exceed the domestic tourists of **every** other major civ, i.e. `visiting_total > max(domestic of each other civ)` (source above).
- Tourism sources: Great Works (table above); wonders +2 each, plus +1 for each later era the owner reaches (data: TOURISM_BASE_FROM_WONDER = 2, TOURISM_ADVANCED_ERA_WONDER = 1; per-era reading from https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Tourism_(Civ6)); religious tourism +8 from the Holy City of a religion you founded (data: TOURISM_FROM_HOLY_CITY); after **Flight**, listed improvements produce tourism equal to their Culture (or Faith/Food for a few uniques) (data: Improvement_Tourism: unique improvements, City Park, Chateau, Pasture, Plantation and others; Seastead and Open Air Museum need no tech); national parks; resorts; rock bands; Great People with district tourism (Masaru Ibuka, Jamsetji Tata, Kenzo Tange); competition rewards; policies.

## Monopolies & Corporations (optional game mode)
Not part of the extracted ruleset (data excludes optional modes). Outline only (engine; unverified): after Economics, Industries are created on improved luxury tiles and later upgraded to Corporations, which produce Products (Great Works) for tourism. Keep optional.

## Score
Running score weights (data: ScoringLineItems.Multiplier): civics 3 each and techs 2 each (both scaled by item cost, engine; unverified how), wonders 15 (cost-scaled), cities 5, districts 2, population 1, great people 5, religion 5, era score 1, era buildings 1, converted cities 2. Score victory at turn limit uses this.
